The Bible Doesn’t Mean Whatever We Want It to Mean

Learning to understand Scripture within its original context instead of making individual verses say what we want them to say.

📖 2 Timothy 2:15Luke 24:13–27Acts 17:10–12Philippians 4:10–13About 60 minutes
Big QuestionHow can we understand what the Bible is actually saying?
Key TruthNever build an entire belief from a verse you have not read in context.

Opening Question

Have you ever quoted a Bible verse and later discovered that it did not mean what you thought it meant?

Introduction

The Bible is the Word of God, but that does not mean every interpretation of the Bible is correct.

Sometimes we pull a sentence from Scripture because it sounds encouraging, supports something we already believe, or fits what we are experiencing. We may sincerely love God and still misunderstand what a passage means.

A verse can be completely true and still be used incorrectly.

The question is not simply, “What does this verse mean to me?” We must first ask, “What was God communicating through the original writer to the original audience?” Only after understanding that can we faithfully apply it to our lives.

Movement 1

Scripture Must Be Handled Carefully

Read 2 Timothy 2:15

Paul instructed Timothy to correctly handle the word of truth. That means Scripture can also be handled incorrectly.

We should not approach the Bible carelessly or use it merely to confirm what we already believe. We must be willing to let Scripture correct us.

Discussion Questions

  • What does it mean to correctly handle Scripture?
  • Why is it tempting to search only for verses that support what we already believe?
  • What is the difference between allowing Scripture to speak and making Scripture agree with us?

Movement 2

Context Helps Us Understand Meaning

Context includes:

  • Who wrote the passage?
  • Who was the original audience?
  • What was happening at the time?
  • What comes before and after the verse?
  • What kind of writing is it?
  • How does it fit within the message of the whole Bible?

Ignoring these questions can cause us to turn a promise made to a particular group into a personal guarantee or treat a proverb as an absolute promise.

Example: Philippians 4:13

“I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me.”

This verse is often used to mean, “With Jesus, I can accomplish anything I want.” But read Philippians 4:10–13.

Paul was discussing contentment. He had experienced hunger and abundance, need and provision. His point was not that Christ would help him achieve every personal goal. His point was that Christ would give him strength to remain faithful in every circumstance.

Discussion Questions

  • How does reading the surrounding verses change our understanding?
  • Is the verse less powerful when read correctly, or does it become more meaningful?
  • What is the difference between achieving anything and enduring anything through Christ?

Movement 3

Jesus Is at the Center of Scripture

Read Luke 24:13–27

The disciples on the road to Emmaus knew the events surrounding Jesus’ death, but they did not understand their meaning. Jesus explained how the Scriptures pointed toward Him.

The Bible is not merely a collection of motivational sayings, isolated promises, and rules. It tells one connected story of creation, human rebellion, redemption, and restoration through Christ.

If our interpretation makes us the center of every passage, we may be missing the larger story.

Discussion Questions

  • Why is it easy to make ourselves the main character of Scripture?
  • How does placing Jesus at the center change how we read the Bible?
  • What happens when we treat the Bible primarily as a collection of inspirational quotes?

Movement 4

We Should Examine What We Are Taught

Read Acts 17:10–12

The Bereans did not accept everything simply because a respected teacher said it. They examined the Scriptures daily to determine whether the teaching was true.

This was not disrespectful. It was spiritual responsibility.

A preacher, teacher, social media post, devotional, or popular Christian saying should never replace reading Scripture for ourselves.

Discussion Questions

  • Why were the Bereans described as having noble character?
  • How can we respectfully examine what a pastor or teacher says?
  • Have you ever heard something that sounded biblical but was not actually in the Bible?

Common Ways We Take Scripture Out of Context

  • Reading only one verse instead of the complete passage
  • Treating every biblical promise as a personal guarantee
  • Confusing descriptive passages with commands
  • Ignoring the original audience and historical situation
  • Using Scripture to support a decision we have already made
  • Depending on social media quotes instead of studying the Bible
  • Asking only, “What does this mean to me?”
  • Making ourselves the center instead of Christ

A Simple Method for Reading Scripture

  1. Observation: What does the passage actually say?
  2. Context: What is happening before and after it?
  3. Meaning: What was the writer communicating to the original audience?
  4. Connection: How does this passage point toward or relate to Christ?
  5. Application: What truth should shape my beliefs, choices, or behavior?

Important Clarification

Context does not make Scripture less personal or less powerful. It protects us from claiming promises God did not make and helps us discover truths we might otherwise miss.

We do not honor the Bible by making it say what we want. We honor it by allowing it to say what God intended.

Personal Reflection

  • Is there a verse I regularly quote without knowing its surrounding passage?
  • Do I approach Scripture seeking truth or simply confirmation?
  • Am I willing to change my beliefs when Scripture challenges them?
  • What can I do this week to become a more careful reader of the Bible?

Weekly Challenge

Choose one verse you frequently quote. Read the entire chapter and write down:

  • Who is speaking
  • Who is being addressed
  • What situation is taking place
  • What the verse means within that passage
  • How it should be applied today

Closing Truth

The Bible does not mean whatever we want it to mean. It means what God intended it to mean. Our responsibility is to study faithfully, understand carefully, and obey humbly.

Closing Prayer

Lord, teach us to handle Your Word faithfully. Keep us from using Scripture merely to support what we already want to believe. Give us wisdom to understand its context, humility to accept correction, and courage to obey what You reveal. Help us see Jesus throughout Scripture and allow Your truth to transform our lives. Amen.
