Accuracy First

A family tree is only as good as its labels.

The Gospel audit keeps the same rules used in the Old Testament. A name in a parable is labeled as a parable character. An angelic being is not inserted into a human genealogy. “Brother,” “son,” legal fatherhood, house/descendant language, textual variants, and possible cross-Gospel identities remain distinguishable instead of being flattened into one modern family-tree assumption.

Scripture-stated

A parent, spouse, child, sibling, relative, household, or other connection is recorded as explicit only when the biblical text states it.

Probability stays labeled

Nathanael/Bartholomew, Thaddaeus/Judas of James, Salome and Zebedee’s household, Cleopas/Clopas, and other likely identities stay marked as probable or possible when the text does not directly equate the names.

Genealogy is not forced

Matthew says Jacob fathered Joseph; Luke records Joseph “of Heli” after saying Jesus was “as was supposed” Joseph’s son. Both Gospel lines are preserved without pretending the Gospels themselves explain the mechanics.
