Beginning with Jesus

Christian faith begins with good news, not with our ability to make ourselves good enough. God came near to us in Jesus Christ, dealt with sin through the cross, defeated death through the resurrection, and invites us into new life with Him.

Key ScripturesJohn 3:16–17 • Mark 1:14–15 • Romans 3:23–24 • Romans 6:23 • Ephesians 2:8–10 • Romans 10:9–10

The gospel in one sentence

The gospel is the good news that God saves sinners through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ and gives new life to everyone who turns to Him in faith.

Christianity is not mainly advice about how to become a better person. It is news about what God has done for us through Jesus. Our response matters, but grace begins with God.

1. God created us for life with Him

You were created by God, in His image, with dignity and purpose. Human beings were made to know God, love Him, reflect His character, and live in right relationship with Him and one another.

Our deepest need is not merely improved circumstances. We need peace with the God who made us.

Read

Genesis 1:26–27 and Acts 17:24–28

2. Sin has separated us from God

Sin is more than making occasional mistakes. It is our rejection of God’s rule, our failure to love Him and others rightly, and our attempt to live life on our own terms. Scripture teaches that every person has sinned. None of us can repair that separation through morality, religion, effort, or comparison with other people.

Sin brings guilt, brokenness, spiritual death, and damage into our relationship with God, ourselves, other people, and creation.

Read

Romans 3:23, Isaiah 53:6, and Romans 6:23

3. Jesus did for us what we could not do

Jesus is not merely a teacher showing us a better path. He is the Son of God who entered human history, lived without sin, revealed the Father, gave His life for us on the cross, and rose from the dead.

At the cross, Jesus took sin seriously and displayed God’s love completely. His resurrection means death did not defeat Him. Jesus is alive, and forgiveness and new life are available through Him.

  • Jesus lived the faithful life we have not lived.
  • Jesus carried the judgment our sin deserves.
  • Jesus rose from death and reigns as Lord.
  • Jesus offers forgiveness, reconciliation, and new life.

Read

1 Corinthians 15:3–4, 2 Corinthians 5:21, and 1 Peter 3:18

4. We respond with repentance and faith

Jesus calls us to repent and believe the good news. Repentance means turning from sin and self-rule toward God. Faith means trusting Jesus, relying on what He has done, and surrendering to Him as Lord.

Repentance is not cleaning yourself up before approaching God. Faith is not pretending you have no questions. You come honestly, trusting that Jesus is sufficient to save and willing to follow where He leads.

Following Jesus includes a decision, but it is more than a moment. It begins a new direction for your whole life.

Read

Mark 1:15, Acts 2:37–39, and Romans 10:9–10

5. Salvation is a gift of grace

You cannot earn salvation. Good works, church attendance, generosity, knowledge, or religious activity cannot purchase God’s forgiveness. We are saved by grace through faith.

Good works matter, but they are the fruit of salvation, not its price. We obey because God has loved and rescued us, not so that He might finally decide to love us.

Read

Ephesians 2:8–10 and Titus 3:3–7

What begins when you trust Christ?

  • You are forgiven. Your sin no longer has the final word.
  • You are reconciled to God. You are welcomed into relationship with Him.
  • You receive new life. God begins changing you from the inside out.
  • You receive the Holy Spirit. God is present to guide, strengthen, convict, and transform you.
  • You become part of God’s family. Following Jesus connects you to His people, the church.
  • You begin a lifelong journey. Growth is real, but it is usually gradual. Grace teaches us to keep walking.

Are you ready to trust Jesus?

You do not become a Christian by repeating perfect words. A prayer cannot save you by itself. Jesus saves. Prayer is simply one way to express genuine repentance and faith.

You can speak honestly to God about your sin, your need, your trust in Jesus, and your desire to follow Him. A prayer may sound like this:

A prayer of responseGod, I know that I have sinned and cannot save myself. I believe Jesus died for my sins and rose from the dead. I turn from living life on my own terms and place my trust in Jesus as Savior and Lord. Forgive me, give me new life, and teach me to follow You. Amen.

Afterward, tell a mature Christian or church leader about your decision. Faith was never designed to grow in isolation.

Pause and consider

  • How would you explain the gospel in your own words?
  • What have you been relying on to make yourself right with God?
  • What does repentance require you to turn from?
  • What makes it difficult for you to trust Jesus fully?
  • Have you personally placed your trust in Christ, or are you still exploring?

Live it today

  1. Read John 3 and Ephesians 2:1–10 slowly.
  2. Write a short paragraph explaining what Jesus has done for you.
  3. Pray honestly, using your own words.
  4. Tell a trusted Christian or church leader where you are in your journey.
  5. Plan to attend a Christ-centered local church this week.

Prayer

Father, thank You for loving me before I understood how much I needed You. Help me see my sin honestly without running from Your grace. Teach me to trust what Jesus has done rather than what I can accomplish. Give me courage to turn from my old direction and follow Christ with my whole life. Lead me into truth, connect me with Your people, and begin Your transforming work in me. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

Step 1 goal

Understand the gospel clearly and identify your personal response to Jesus.
