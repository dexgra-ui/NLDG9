Beyond the Label

Faithful in the Everyday

Designed by God. Not defined by the world.

Whatever you do, whether in word or deed, do it all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him.Colossians 3:17 (NIV)

A Message from Dexter

Sometimes I think we make following Jesus more complicated than it needs to be. We look for the extraordinary moments, the big assignments, the opportunities to do something impressive. But what about the ordinary things?

What about how we treat someone when we disagree? How we serve when nobody is watching? How we respond when life doesn't go according to plan?

As we enter November, I've been thinking about faithfulness and gratitude. Not just the kind we talk about during Thanksgiving, but the kind that shapes how we live throughout the year. We often thank God for what He's given us. And we should. But perhaps we should also ask what we're doing with what we've been given.

That's part of what No Labels, Designed by God is about. Recognizing that every person has value, every gift has a purpose, and every day presents opportunities to reflect the character of Jesus. Faithfulness doesn't always require something extraordinary. Sometimes it simply requires being available.

Featured Reflection: The Red-Handled Screwdriver

After years of working as a technician, one of my most dependable tools is still an ordinary red-handled Phillips screwdriver. It isn't fancy. It doesn't do everything. But it does what it was designed to do.

That got me thinking about the body of Christ. In 1 Corinthians 12, Paul reminds us that we have different gifts, but every part of the body matters.

Maybe we should stop comparing ourselves to the person beside us and start asking whether we're being faithful with what God has placed in our hands.

Walking with Jesus: Faithfulness in Everyday Encounters

One thing that continues to stand out as we walk through the Gospels is how often Jesus used ordinary encounters to reveal extraordinary truths. A conversation. A meal. A question. A person others might have overlooked.

Jesus didn't limit His ministry to religious gatherings or special occasions. He met people where they were. And those encounters challenge us to think about our own lives.

Are we paying attention to the people around us? Do we recognize opportunities to listen, encourage, or show compassion? Are we willing to let someone else's needs interrupt our plans?

Following Jesus isn't simply about knowing what He taught. It's about allowing His example to shape how we live.

Faithfulness When Nobody Is Watching

It's easy to associate faithfulness with visible service: teaching a class, leading worship, organizing an event, doing something others recognize.

But much of life happens outside the spotlight. It's the patience we show at home, the integrity we demonstrate at work, the kindness we offer someone who cannot do anything for us in return. Those moments may never receive public recognition. That doesn't make them insignificant.

Colossians 3:17 reminds us to do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God through Him. Not just the impressive things. Everything.

When Gratitude Becomes Action

Thanksgiving gives us an opportunity to pause and recognize what we've received. But perhaps it should also challenge us to examine what we're doing with what we've been given.

It's one thing to thank God for our families. It's another to demonstrate love, patience, and compassion toward them. It's one thing to thank God for our communities. It's another to recognize the people within those communities who need encouragement, support, or simply someone willing to listen.

James 2:15–17 challenges us to consider what happens when we recognize someone's needs but offer only words. That doesn't mean we can solve every problem or meet every need. It means gratitude should help us recognize opportunities to act.

Maybe one of the most meaningful ways to give thanks is to become a reason someone else has something to be thankful for.

A Question to Carry With You

Am I so focused on doing something extraordinary for God that I'm overlooking the ordinary opportunities He's already placed before me?

And how might my gratitude become something another person can actually experience?

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for the gifts, opportunities, and people You've placed in our lives.

Help us recognize that faithfulness isn't measured by how visible our service is. Teach us to be grateful not only in our words, but also in our actions.

Give us patience when we're interrupted, compassion when someone is struggling, and humility when we need to listen. Help us recognize opportunities to serve without seeking recognition.

May our everyday lives reflect the love and character of Jesus. In His name, amen.

Until Next Time

Don't just inherit your faith. Examine it.
