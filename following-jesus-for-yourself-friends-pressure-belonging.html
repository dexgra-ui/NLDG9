Preteen & Teen Bible Studies · Lesson 6 of 10

Friends, Pressure, and Belonging.

Belonging is a real human need. Following Jesus means learning how to receive friendship as a gift without trading conviction, wisdom, or dignity for approval.

📖 Proverbs 13:20Daniel 1:3–20Galatians 1:101 Corinthians 15:3335–50 minutes

Big Question

How do I belong without letting other people decide who I become?

Big Truth

Friendship is a gift, and every close relationship carries influence.

Following Jesus means choosing friends wisely, recognizing pressure, setting boundaries, and refusing to trade conviction for approval.

Open Your Bible

Pay attention to influence and courage.

  1. Proverbs 13:20 and 1 Corinthians 15:33 — Notice what Scripture says about companions and influence.
  2. Daniel 1:3–20 — Watch Daniel and his friends face pressure with both conviction and wisdom.
  3. Galatians 1:10 — Ask what happens when pleasing people becomes our highest goal.

1

Wanting to belong is not weakness.

People are made for relationship. Friendship, family, teams, classes, churches, and communities can give encouragement and support.

The danger begins when belonging becomes so important that we ignore truth, violate conscience, mistreat others, or disobey God to keep our place.

2

Pressure is not always loud.

Peer pressure can be an obvious demand, but it can also look like laughing because everyone else laughs, staying silent while someone is humiliated, joining gossip, posting for approval, or pretending to agree so no one thinks you are strange.

Pressure often works through the fear of losing belonging.

3

Daniel shows conviction without unnecessary disrespect.

Daniel lived under authority in a culture that did not share his beliefs. He did not become rude, reckless, or arrogant. He made a clear request, proposed a wise test, and remained faithful.

Courage is not the same as being argumentative. Sometimes the strongest response is calm, respectful, and firm.

4

Friends influence direction over time.

Scripture does not tell Christians to know only people exactly like them. Jesus interacted with many kinds of people. But close relationships shape habits, language, expectations, courage, and what begins to feel normal.

Ask not only, “Do I like being around this person?” but also, “Who am I becoming when I spend a lot of time in this relationship?”

5

Healthy friendship includes truth and boundaries.

A real friend should not require you to hide, lie, break rules, mock others, misuse substances, send private images, keep dangerous secrets, or violate your convictions to prove loyalty.

You can care about someone and still say no. You can forgive and still use boundaries. You can be kind without joining everything another person does.

Think It Through

What can courage look like in a group?

If everyone begins making fun of someone who is not there, faithful responses might include refusing to join, changing the subject, defending the person, leaving, or speaking privately to a friend afterward.

If someone says, “If you were really my friend, you would do this,” why might that sentence be a warning sign?

Talk About It

Questions for discussion.

  1. What does Proverbs 13:20 teach about close influence?
  2. What kind of pressure did Daniel face?
  3. How did Daniel show both conviction and wisdom?
  4. What are subtle forms of peer pressure?
  5. How can you tell whether a friendship is helping or harming character?
  6. What is the difference between loyalty and control?
  7. How can you disagree with friends without becoming self-righteous?

Do not name or criticize real classmates or friends in a group. You may use general examples or pass on a question.

Preteen Track · roughly 11–14

CLOSE · FRIENDLY · CAREFUL

Draw three circles with those labels. Talk about why not every person belongs at the same level of trust. Some are close friends, some are people you enjoy but do not know deeply, and some relationships need extra boundaries because of repeated harmful choices.

Boundaries are not a way to label anyone as worthless. They are a way to use wisdom about trust and influence.

Teen Deeper Dive · roughly 14–18

How groups make things feel normal.

Discuss belonging in friend groups, teams, dating relationships, workplaces, political spaces, fandoms, and online communities.

How can a group slowly make something unhealthy feel normal? What is the difference between being countercultural for attention and being faithful when conviction genuinely costs something?

Try It This Week

Pray about one relationship.

Ask what good influence the person brings, what influence you bring, whether pressure needs to be recognized, and whether a boundary, apology, encouragement, or honest conversation should be considered.

Do not make a dramatic decision because of one lesson. Seek wisdom from a trusted adult when a friendship situation is complicated.

Memory Verse

Galatians 1:10

Learn the verse from the Bible translation your family, church, or class uses.

Prayer

Become a faithful friend.

Jesus, thank You for the gift of friendship and community. Help me become the kind of friend who tells the truth, keeps wise boundaries, encourages what is good, and refuses to use people. Give me courage when belonging feels more important than obedience. Teach me to be kind without being controlled and confident without becoming proud. Amen.

Leader / Parent Note

Teach influence without creating an “us versus them” attitude.

Purpose: Jesus calls His followers to love people while also walking wisely.

No group gossip

Do not ask students to identify “bad friends” publicly or invite speculation about another young person’s character.

Safety concerns

If a student describes threats, coercion, stalking, pressure to send sexual images, abuse, exploitation, substance danger, or another serious concern, involve an appropriate trusted adult and follow safeguarding procedures and applicable reporting requirements. Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep.

Forgiveness and trust

Do not equate forgiveness with immediate restoration of trust. Trust may need time, distance, or adult intervention.

This study does not ask young people to submit names, private relationship details, messages, photos, schools, locations, or contact information.
