# Expanded Leader Guide
## AI and Human Uniqueness

### Purpose
Equip participants to ground human dignity in God’s image, understand AI without hype or panic, and practice truthful, responsible, neighbor-loving technology use.

### Before You Teach
- Read Genesis 1:26–28, Psalm 8, Psalm 139:13–18, James 3:9, and Colossians 1:15–17.
- Identify your own posture toward AI: excitement, fear, skepticism, dependence, or uncertainty.
- Choose one ordinary AI example your group will recognize.
- Do not let technical discussion replace the biblical objective.
- Pray that the group will honor people rather than debate machines.

### Biblical Background
Genesis gives image-bearing dignity and responsibility to humanity, not only to kings, elites, or highly capable people. Christian traditions emphasize relational, moral, representative, rational, creative, and vocational dimensions of the image. Do not reduce the image to intelligence alone. Sin distorts but does not erase dignity. Jesus is the perfect image of the invisible God and shows what faithful humanity looks like.

### Theological Themes
Creation, dignity, stewardship, sin, wisdom, embodiment, accountability, community, and discipleship.

### Biblical Worldview
Humans are created by God and for God. Technology is created by humans and must remain accountable to God’s purposes. AI may support useful work, accessibility, communication, health care, research, and creativity. It may also spread falsehood, expose private information, reproduce injustice, hide authorship, weaken skills, or concentrate power. Christians should test claims, preserve accountability, protect people, and ask how decisions affect those with the least power.

### Teaching Outline
1. Human dignity is received from God.
2. Human worth is deeper than performance.
3. AI imitates abilities but does not confer dignity.
4. Tools extend human intentions and power.
5. Christians use technology through love and wisdom.

### Suggested Timeline
**45 minutes:** welcome 5, Scripture 7, image of God 10, AI and stewardship 8, discussion 10, prayer 5.  
**60 minutes:** welcome 7, Scripture 10, theology 12, worldview and culture 10, discussion 15, prayer 6.  
**90 minutes:** welcome 10, Scripture observation 15, background 15, opportunities and risks 15, case studies 15, discussion 12, application and prayer 8.

### Difficult Questions
- **Can AI become human or bear God’s image?** Scripture grounds image-bearing in God’s creation of humanity, not in achieving a level of intelligence.
- **Can AI think or feel?** Systems can imitate reasoning and emotional language. Claims about inner awareness remain debated and should be stated cautiously.
- **Is using AI cheating?** It depends on the agreement, assignment, disclosure, and purpose. Secret assistance when original work is required is dishonest.
- **Should pastors use AI?** It may assist organization or research leads, but it cannot replace prayer, interpretation, pastoral responsibility, congregational knowledge, or verification.
- **Can AI disciple someone?** It may deliver information, but discipleship includes embodied community, example, correction, worship, accountability, and sacrificial love.
- **Will AI take jobs?** Avoid presenting predictions as certainty. Christians should care about workers, training, fair transition, economic dignity, and the distribution of gains and burdens.

### Discussion Coaching
Affirm enthusiasm while asking who remains accountable. Acknowledge fear without feeding panic. Redirect technical debates by asking, “How does this affect obedience to God and love for people?”

### Common Misunderstandings
The image of God is physical appearance; intelligence alone defines the image; technology is neutral in every sense; AI removes user responsibility; rejecting every new tool is automatically faithful.

### Practical Ministry
Protect confidential information. Verify generated claims and Scripture references. Teach clear schoolwork and authorship standards. Preserve human review in decisions affecting people. Use technology to improve access without replacing care. Consider workers and vulnerable communities.

### Prayer Focus
Pray for wisdom without fear, innovation without pride, truth without deception, productivity without dehumanization, and technology that serves people rather than mastering them.

### Leader Reflection
Where do I measure worth by productivity? Where do I trust efficiency more than faithfulness? Have I used technology in a way I would not disclose? Whose vulnerability might I overlook? What must I model before teaching this lesson?
