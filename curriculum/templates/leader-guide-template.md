# Expanded Leader Guide Template

## Lesson snapshot

- Curriculum and version
- Lesson number and title
- Primary and supporting Scripture
- Big idea
- Main objective
- Learning objectives
- Memory verse
- Recommended audience
- Preparation level
- Suggested 45-, 60-, and 90-minute plans

## Before you teach

Invite the leader to read the passage repeatedly, pray, identify personal obedience, and consider the needs and safety of the group.

> The goal is not to finish a lesson. The goal is to become more like Jesus and help others do the same.

## Biblical and historical background

Explain only context that clarifies the passage:

- Literary setting
- Historical setting
- Cultural practices
- Geography
- Political or religious conditions
- People and timeline

Identify uncertainty honestly and avoid presenting speculation as fact.

## Passage walkthrough

Divide the primary text into sections. For each section include:

1. What the text says
2. What it means in context
3. What it teaches about God, Jesus, humanity, sin, grace, discipleship, holiness, or mission
4. What leaders should emphasize
5. A transition or discussion prompt

## Theological themes

Explain the major doctrines in clear language. Show how the lesson fits the broader biblical story of creation, fall, covenant, redemption, church, mission, and new creation where relevant.

## Scripture connections

Include meaningful Old and New Testament cross-references. Explain the connection rather than listing references without guidance.

## Biblical worldview

Answer:

- What does Scripture clearly affirm?
- What assumptions should Christians question?
- What wisdom is needed today?
- Where should Christians resist fear, pride, control, indifference, or false hope?

## Current culture

Describe the modern issue accurately and fairly. Distinguish facts, interpretations, trends, and disputed claims. Avoid partisan framing unless the lesson directly requires analysis of a public-policy question.

## Teaching outline

Provide a ready-to-teach structure:

- Introduction
- Point one, explanation, illustration, application
- Point two, explanation, illustration, application
- Point three, explanation, illustration, application
- Conclusion and invitation to respond

## Discussion coaching

For every major question provide:

- Purpose of the question
- Likely responses
- Follow-up prompts
- A way to redirect debate toward Scripture
- Guidance for quieter participants

## Difficult questions and common misunderstandings

Include concise, biblically grounded responses. State when Scripture does not directly answer a question. Encourage further study rather than false certainty.

## Safety and pastoral care

Identify subjects that may involve abuse, trauma, mental health, self-harm, addiction, medical care, legal obligations, children, vulnerable adults, or mandatory reporting. Direct leaders toward appropriate pastoral, professional, or emergency support.

## Practical ministry application

Address only relevant settings:

- Personal discipleship
- Marriage and family
- Church and small groups
- Work and school
- Community and public witness
- Mentoring and leadership

## Living the Greatest Commandments

- How does this truth deepen love for God?
- How should it change the way we love our neighbor?

## Weekly challenge and prayer focus

Assign one clear act of obedience and one focused prayer emphasis.

## Leader reflection

- What challenged me?
- Where must I obey before teaching?
- Who may need special care?
- What must remain central if time is limited?
- How will I know the lesson moved toward transformation?

## Resource connections

Include verified and directly relevant internal resources and a limited number of trustworthy external resources when needed.

## Review checklist

- Scripture governs the lesson.
- Jesus and the gospel remain central.
- Claims are accurate and appropriately qualified.
- Participant and leader guides agree.
- Questions move from observation to obedience.
- Applications are specific but not manipulative.
- Safety guidance is present where necessary.
- The lesson can be taught by a developing leader.
