Accuracy First

A family tree is only as good as its labels.

Some biblical names clearly identify individual people. Others function as ancestor names, nations, clans, return-family labels, titles, literary designations, musical headings, symbolic figures, or leadership names. Some relationships are explicit; a few are probable from immediate context; manuscript and translation traditions sometimes preserve variant forms. The prophetic books require the same care so imagery, divine or angelic names, place names, royal titles, or traditional identifications are not quietly converted into Scripture-stated human people.

Scripture-stated

A parent, spouse, child, sibling, concubine, clan, or other connection is recorded as explicit only when the biblical text states it.

Probability stays labeled

When a relationship or identity is strongly suggested but not directly worded, the record says probable or unresolved instead of pretending the inference is a quotation from Scripture.

Variants and identities stay visible

Textual variants, compressed genealogies, different name traditions, literary titles, repeated-name questions, and prophetic symbols are not “fixed” by choosing one spelling or merging two people without evidence. The uncertainty remains part of the reference.
