Leading Like Jesus

Christian leadership serves, protects, listens, tells the truth, accepts accountability, develops others, and refuses domination.

📖 Mark 10:42–45; John 13:1–17Supporting: Philippians 2:3–8; 1 Peter 5:2–345–60 minutesService, authority, and responsibility

Central biblical truth

Jesus redefines greatness through service and sacrifice. Leadership is not entitlement to control people. It is responsibility exercised with humility, truth, protection, accountability, and love.

Study introduction

Men are often told to lead, but not always taught what leadership under Jesus looks like. Without Christ’s example, leadership can become volume, title, control, decision-making without listening, or using responsibility to avoid accountability.

Jesus held real authority and used it to serve, protect, teach, correct, wash feet, and give His life.

Scripture focus

Read Mark 10:42–45 and John 13:1–17. Notice:

  • How worldly rulers use authority
  • How Jesus defines greatness
  • What Jesus knew about His authority before washing feet
  • What service does and does not remove
  • How His example becomes a command

1. Authority is stewardship, not ownership

A leader does not own the people he serves. Spouses, children, employees, volunteers, and church members remain persons with dignity, agency, gifts, and responsibilities before God.

Authority should have limits, oversight, and accountability. A man who cannot be questioned is not leading like Jesus.

2. Service is not weakness or avoidance

Jesus served while still teaching truth, confronting sin, setting direction, and making difficult decisions.

Servant leadership does not mean pleasing everyone, refusing conflict, doing every task personally, or allowing harmful behavior to continue. It means power is used for the good of others rather than personal ego.

3. Listening is part of responsibility

Good leaders seek information before deciding. They listen to people affected by the decision, especially those with less power.

Listening does not guarantee agreement. It demonstrates respect and helps expose blind spots, fear, missing facts, and unintended harm.

4. Leaders repair harm

When a leader is wrong, position does not remove the need to repent. Repair may include apology, correction, restitution, changed policy, restored access to information, or stepping back from a role.

“I am the leader” is never a biblical excuse for intimidation, abuse, secrecy, or refusing consequences.

Conversation questions

  1. How does Jesus contrast worldly and kingdom leadership?
  2. What does foot washing reveal about authority?
  3. Why is listening part of leadership?
  4. What is the difference between service and people-pleasing?
  5. How can leaders invite accountability?
  6. What forms of control are sometimes mislabeled leadership?
  7. What does repair require when a leader causes harm?
  8. Where can you use influence for another person’s good?

Pause and consider

Live it this week

Practice 1: Ask before assuming

Ask someone affected by your leadership what support, clarity, or change would help.

Practice 2: Serve without announcement

Complete one needed task that protects or supports others without seeking recognition.

Practice 3: Invite correction

Ask a trusted person where your leadership becomes unclear, distant, controlling, or avoidant.

Prayer

Jesus, You are Lord and Servant. Purify my use of influence.

Teach me to listen, protect, tell the truth, make wise decisions, accept correction, and repair harm. Keep me from domination, pride, passivity, and the need to be praised. Help me use responsibility for the good of others and Your glory.

Amen.

Leader and mentor notes

Main objective

Replace control-based leadership with Christlike service, accountability, courage, listening, and repair.

Safety response

Abuse, coercive control, threats, sexual misconduct, financial exploitation, or harm to children and vulnerable adults require appropriate reporting, safeguarding, and qualified support.

Avoid

  • Teaching male authority as immunity from correction
  • Using submission language to excuse abuse
  • Assuming every man holds the same family or church role
  • Calling passivity humility
  • Restoring leadership before accountability and demonstrated change

Suggested closing question

Who becomes safer, stronger, or more mature because of the way you lead?
