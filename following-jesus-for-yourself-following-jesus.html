Following Jesus for Yourself.

Family and church can introduce you to Jesus and help you grow. But no one else can trust, follow, or obey Him in your place.

📖 Matthew 16:13–25Luke 9:23–25John 15:1–1735–50 minutesAges 11–18

Big Question

What happens when faith stops being only something my family or church believes and becomes something I must respond to personally?

Big Truth

No one can follow Jesus for you.

Family, pastors, teachers, and friends can guide and encourage you, but each person must respond to Jesus personally. Following Him means trusting who He is, receiving His grace, learning His ways, and choosing daily to live under His leadership.

Open Your Bible

Move from public opinion to personal response.

  1. Matthew 16:13–25 — Notice the change from “Who do people say I am?” to “Who do you say I am?”
  2. Luke 9:23–25 — Pay attention to daily, follow, and lose/save. Jesus does not describe discipleship as an occasional interest.
  3. John 15:1–17 — Look for remaining, fruit, love, obedience, and friendship with Jesus.

1

Borrowed faith can introduce you to Jesus, but it cannot replace your response.

Many people first hear about God through parents, grandparents, church, school, camp, or friends. That can be a gift. Eventually, though, each person must ask: What do I actually believe about Jesus, and what will I do with that belief?

Jesus asked His disciples what other people were saying, then He asked them directly. Following Jesus includes learning to answer that question personally.

2

Following Jesus is more than agreeing with facts.

A person can know Christian vocabulary, attend church, and understand Bible stories without allowing Jesus to lead their life.

Discipleship reaches priorities, relationships, honesty, money, time, forgiveness, sexuality, ambition, technology, speech, and the way we treat people who cannot do anything for us.

3

Grace comes first, but grace teaches us to follow.

We do not earn salvation by proving we are serious enough. The gospel is good news because salvation is God’s gift through Christ.

Grace is not permission to keep Jesus at a safe distance. Following Him includes surrender: “Jesus, You are Lord. My preferences do not outrank You.”

4

Following Jesus is a relationship that must be nourished.

John 15 pictures branches remaining in a vine. A branch does not create life by trying harder to look alive; it receives life by remaining connected.

Scripture, prayer, worship, fellowship, confession, service, and obedience do not make God love us. They help us remain attentive to Christ and grow in the life He gives.

Think It Through

Personal does not mean isolated.

“My whole family follows Jesus, so I guess I do too” misses personal response. But “Faith is personal, so I do not need church, wisdom, correction, or anyone else” misses Christian community.

Biblical faith is personal, but not isolated. We respond to Jesus ourselves and grow among other believers.

Talk About It

Questions for discussion.

  1. Why do you think Jesus asked, “Who do you say I am?”
  2. What is the difference between knowing about Jesus and following Jesus?
  3. How can family faith be a gift without becoming a substitute for personal faith?
  4. Which parts of life are easiest to give Jesus? Which are harder?
  5. What does “take up your cross daily” mean in ordinary life?
  6. What does John 15 teach about remaining in Jesus?
  7. Why do Christians still need community if faith is personal?

You may answer generally or pass on a question. Personal discipleship should never be turned into forced public disclosure.

Preteen Track · roughly 11–14

What can others do, and what must I choose?

Make two columns. Under “Things other people can do for me,” include teach Scripture, take me to church, pray for me, answer questions, and encourage me. Under “Things I must choose,” include tell the truth, pray honestly, ask questions, admit when I am wrong, choose kindness, and trust Jesus.

Talk about why both columns matter.

Teen Deeper Dive · roughly 14–18

Cultural Christianity or discipleship?

A person can be surrounded by Christian language while success, approval, pleasure, politics, image, or comfort functions as the real center.

Ask: If someone watched my choices but never heard my religious words, what would they think has the most authority in my life?

Try It This Week

Practice one daily rhythm for seven days.

  • Read a short Gospel passage and ask, “What does this show me about Jesus?”
  • Pray honestly for five minutes without trying to sound impressive.
  • Identify one area where you know what obedience looks like and practice it.
  • Ask a trusted Christian adult one real question about following Jesus.

Memory Verse

Luke 9:23

Learn the verse from the Bible translation your family, church, or class uses.

Prayer

Respond personally.

Jesus, I do not want to follow You only because other people around me do. Help me know You, trust You, and respond to You personally. Thank You for Your grace. Teach me to surrender what needs to change, remain close to You, and follow You in ordinary choices. Give me courage to ask honest questions and humility to receive wise guidance. Amen.

Leader / Parent Note

Invite response without manufacturing it.

Purpose: Help young people distinguish inherited religious environment from personal discipleship without disrespecting families or churches that have faithfully taught them.

No pressure for a public decision

Do not pressure a student into making a public profession, emotional decision, baptism commitment, or denominational commitment in front of a group. Invite thoughtful response and private follow-up with trusted church leadership when appropriate.

Respect across traditions

Where Christian traditions differ about baptism, confirmation, communion, or membership, explain your church’s teaching honestly without presenting other faithful traditions as enemies. Keep the lesson centered on responding to Jesus.

Safeguarding

If a young person raises a safety concern or discloses abuse, self-harm, exploitation, or danger, follow your organization’s safeguarding procedures and applicable reporting requirements. Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep.

This study does not ask young people to create an account or submit personal information, private stories, prayer requests, names, ages, schools, locations, photos, or contact details.
