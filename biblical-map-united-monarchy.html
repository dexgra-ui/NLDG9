Biblical Maps • 1 Samuel–1 Kings 11

Israel Under Saul, David & Solomon

Follow the shift from Saul’s tribal monarchy to David’s united kingdom and Solomon’s wider political and economic reach. The map distinguishes the kingdom’s core land from surrounding peoples and broader zones of tribute, alliance, or influence.

Accuracy note: Ancient kingdoms did not function with modern surveyed national borders. The biblical texts describe cities, battles, tribute, vassal relationships, administrative districts, alliances, and spheres of control. NLDG therefore uses broad overlays for the Israelite core and dashed zones for surrounding influence rather than drawing a single hard border and calling it exact.
Rule, tribute, and influence are not identical. David’s victories over neighboring peoples and Solomon’s reach toward Hamath and the Euphrates are real claims of the biblical text, but those descriptions should not automatically be visualized as direct day-to-day administration over every square mile. The larger overlay is labeled as a broad biblical sphere of influence/tribute, not a modern sovereign boundary.

Map key

Three kinds of geography.

Israelite core

Broad land associated with the united tribal kingdom from Dan toward Beersheba and across portions of Transjordan.

Wider influence / tribute

Neighboring zones connected to Davidic conquest, tributary relationships, Solomon’s reach, or strategic control.

Royal / strategic site

Capital or key location in the monarchy narrative.

Alliance / external center

Important neighboring city whose relationship is not represented as Israelite core territory.

Royal geography

The kingdom changes as the kings change.

Saul • Gibeah

Saul’s royal center was associated with Gibeah in Benjamin. Tell el-Ful is a common modern proposal, though OpenBible rates the identification only medium confidence.

1 Samuel 10:26; 15:34

David • Hebron

David first ruled Judah from Hebron for seven and a half years before the northern tribes joined him.

2 Samuel 2:1–4; 5:1–5

David & Solomon • Jerusalem

David captured Jerusalem and established it as the political capital. Solomon later built the Temple and royal complex there.

2 Samuel 5:6–10; 1 Kings 5–8

Tyre

Hiram of Tyre maintained an important alliance and supplied materials and craftsmen for Davidic and Solomonic building projects.

2 Samuel 5:11; 1 Kings 5

Transjordan & Edom

David’s campaigns brought Moab, Ammon, and Edom into varying forms of subordination or conflict; these are shown as neighboring influence zones rather than permanent hard borders.

2 Samuel 8–12

North toward Hamath

The biblical accounts describe Davidic victories in Syria and Solomon’s rule or influence extending northward, including Hamath and toward the Euphrates.

2 Samuel 8:3–12; 1 Kings 4:21,24; 2 Chronicles 8:3–4

Reference approach

Broad influence, not invented borders.

The era overlay is cross-checked with Bible Mapper’s “The Kingdoms of Saul, David, and Solomon” and “Solomon’s Administrative Districts.” Bible Mapper likewise distinguishes the expanding monarchy from the earlier tribal period and treats Solomon’s organization as administrative districts rather than simply reusing tribal borders. Key locations are cross-checked with OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding, including Jerusalem, Hebron, Tel el-Ful as a Gibeah proposal, Tyre, Dan, and Beersheba.
