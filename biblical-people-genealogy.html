Accuracy First

A family tree is only as good as its labels.

Some biblical names clearly identify individual people. Others function as ancestor names, nations, clans, return-family labels, titles, literary designations, musical headings, or leadership names. Some relationships are explicit; a few are probable from immediate context; manuscript and translation traditions sometimes preserve variant forms. The prophetic books require the same care so a symbolic name, place, divine title, military title, or poetic image is not quietly converted into a Scripture-stated person.

Scripture-stated

A parent, spouse, child, sibling, concubine, clan, or other connection is recorded as explicit only when the biblical text states it.

Probability stays labeled

When a relationship or identity is strongly suggested but not directly worded, the record says probable or unresolved instead of pretending the inference is a quotation from Scripture.

Variants and identities stay visible

Textual variants, compressed genealogies, different name traditions, literary titles, repeated-name questions, and prophetic symbols are not “fixed” by choosing one spelling or merging two people without evidence. The uncertainty remains part of the reference.

Roadmap

This becomes the whole-Bible reference one verified section at a time.

Phase 1 · Genesis 1–11

Primeval families, Noah, Table of Nations, Shem to Terah. Entered.

Phase 2 · Genesis 12–50

Patriarchal families, Edom and Seir, Joseph, and the family entering Egypt. Entered.

Phase 3 · Exodus–Deuteronomy

Moses’ generation, Aaronic and Levitical lines, tribal leaders, spies, census families, wilderness figures, and land-allocation leaders. Entered.

Phase 4 · Joshua–Ruth

Conquest-era leaders, named judges and rulers, Samson, the Danite priestly line, and Ruth’s family line into David. Entered.

Phase 5 · 1 Samuel–2 Kings

Samuel and Eli’s families, Saul’s house, David’s family, Judah and Israel’s royal dynasties, prophets, officials, warriors, Assyrian-era rulers, and the Babylonian fall of Jerusalem. Entered.

Phase 6 · 1 Chronicles–2 Chronicles

The concentrated genealogy audit: tribal families, expanded Judah and Benjamin lines, Saul’s descendants, David’s household, Levites, priests, temple personnel, royal officials, and alternate textual traditions. Entered.

Phase 7 · Ezra–Esther

Return families, Ezra’s priestly line, Nehemiah’s wall and covenant networks, the post-exile high priests, Jerusalem residents, and Esther’s Persian court. Entered.

Phase 8 · Job–Song of Songs

Job’s named circle, Psalms’ historical and superscription names, Proverbs’ named sages, and a strict audit of Ecclesiastes and Song of Songs that does not manufacture names from titles or traditional identifications. Entered.

Phase 9 · Isaiah–Daniel

Isaiah entered. Next: Jeremiah and Lamentations, followed by Ezekiel and Daniel, including named prophets and relatives, kings and foreign rulers, priestly and court officials, Jeremiah’s network, Ezekiel’s stated ancestry, Daniel and his companions, and historical names embedded in the major prophetic books.
