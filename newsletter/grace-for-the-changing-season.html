Beyond the Label

Grace for the Changing Season

Designed by God. Not defined by the world.

“And even to your old age I am he; and even to hoar hairs will I carry you.”Isaiah 46:4

A Message from Dexter

September has a way of reminding us that seasons change.

The days begin getting shorter. Summer starts giving way to fall. Schedules change. Children return to school. Families settle into new routines. Before long, another year will be moving toward its final months.

Life has seasons too.

Some arrive with excitement. Others come with questions. Some seasons we gladly step into, while others leave us looking back at what used to be.

That has been on my mind lately.

There is a kind of grief that can come with change. We watch our children grow. We watch people we love grow older. We remember places, relationships, opportunities, and moments that once felt permanent. Eventually we discover that life keeps moving whether we are ready for the next chapter or not.

But Scripture reminds us of something important.

Our seasons change.

God does not.

The same God who walked with us through yesterday is already present in tomorrow.

Maybe faith is not always about asking God to keep life from changing. Sometimes faith is trusting that when the season changes, God will still be there.

And maybe part of growing spiritually is learning not only to thank God for where we have been, but also to ask:

Lord, what are You doing in me now?

Because no matter what season of life we find ourselves in, our story is not finished simply because one chapter has ended.

We are still being shaped. Still being called. Still walking with Jesus.

Still designed by God, not defined by the world.

Thoughts from the Ride: The Grief of Aging

Recently, I heard Steve Burns, formerly of Blue’s Clues, talking about “the grief of aging” on his podcast, Alive with Steve Burns. That phrase stopped me in my tracks.

I had never heard aging described that way before. But the more I thought about it, the more it made sense.

As we get older, there is a certain grief that comes with watching life change. Not necessarily because life is bad, but because seasons pass.

Children grow up. Parents grow older. Friends move away. People we love are no longer here. Opportunities come and go. Places that once felt familiar begin to look different. We eventually realize that some chapters of our lives have ended, and others are moving closer to their final pages.

Sometimes we feel a sadness that is difficult to explain. We are not mourning a recent death. We are not necessarily depressed. We may even be grateful for our lives and everything God has given us.

Yet something feels different.

Maybe what we are feeling is grief.

It is the grief of realizing how quickly time passes. It is the grief of saying goodbye to seasons we loved, even when the season ahead may also be good. It is the grief of remembering people, places, and moments we cannot return to.

At this stage of my life, I understand that more clearly. My children are grown. I am watching a new generation come behind me. People who once cared for me are gone, and my own body reminds me that I am not as young as I once was.

I am grateful for where I am, but I would be lying if I said I never missed what used to be.

The strange thing about aging is that joy and grief often exist together. You can be proud of the adults your children have become while still missing the days when they were little. You can be thankful for the wisdom you have gained while missing the energy you once had. You can appreciate your life today while quietly grieving a version of life that no longer exists.

But there is another side to aging.

There is wisdom where there was once uncertainty. There is perspective where there was once urgency. There is patience where there was once frustration. There is gratitude for things we once overlooked because we assumed they would always be there.

Perhaps the goal is not to avoid the grief. Maybe we should allow ourselves to acknowledge it. Grief is not always evidence that something is wrong. Sometimes it is evidence that we have loved deeply, lived fully, and finally learned what truly matters.

Scripture tells us that there is a season for everything.

“To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven.”Ecclesiastes 3:1

Every season has something to teach us, but no season was meant to last forever. The challenge is learning not to hold so tightly to what was that we fail to recognize what God is doing now.

Faith does not require us to pretend that change does not hurt. We can thank God for the past, grieve what has ended, appreciate what remains, and still trust Him with what comes next.

Aging is not just about growing older. It is about learning to release what we cannot keep, recognizing the value of the present moment, and understanding that every day, every relationship, and every season is a gift.

Perhaps aging teaches us how to hold gratitude and grief in the same hand, while trusting God with both.

Go Deeper: The Grief of Aging

Main Scripture: Ecclesiastes 3:1–11

Big Question: How can we grieve the seasons that have passed without missing what God is doing in the season we are living now?

The companion Bible study takes this reflection into Scripture, prayer, and conversation. It explores the quiet losses that come with aging, the wisdom of numbering our days, God’s faithfulness as our bodies and roles change, and the purpose we still carry into every season.

Grief says, “That season mattered.”
Gratitude says, “Thank You, God, for allowing me to experience it.”
Faith says, “Although that season has ended, I trust You with the one I am living now.”

Scripture for the Month

“And even to your old age I am he; and even to hoar hairs will I carry you.”Isaiah 46:4

God’s faithfulness is not limited to the years when we are strongest or most productive. He carried us before, He carries us now, and He will remain faithful through every season still ahead.

Seasons change. Bodies change. Relationships change. Responsibilities change.

God remains faithful.

Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for every season You have allowed us to experience. Thank You for the people, places, relationships, and moments that have shaped our lives.

Help us grieve honestly when seasons end. Teach us to remember the past with gratitude without becoming trapped there. Give us wisdom to recognize what You are doing in our lives today.

Remind us that although our circumstances change, Your faithfulness does not. Keep shaping us, keep calling us, and help us use what You have taught us to encourage those coming behind us.

Teach us to value the present, trust You with what comes next, and keep walking with Jesus.

In Jesus’ name, amen.
