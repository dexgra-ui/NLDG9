God’s Word Helps Me Grow.

The Bible is not a pile of random sayings. It tells God’s story and teaches us to know Him, understand truth, and live faithfully.

📖 Psalm 119:9–16, 1052 Timothy 3:14–17Luke 24:25–2720–30 minutesAges 7–11

Big Question

Why did God give us the Bible, and how can it help us grow?

Big Truth

The Bible is God’s Word.

It tells the true story of who God is, what He has done, how sin has affected the world, how God saves through Jesus, and how His people are called to live. We grow by reading Scripture carefully, understanding it in context, and putting God’s truth into practice.

Open Your Bible

Read and notice.

  1. Psalm 119:9–16 and 105 — Notice how the writer describes God’s Word.
  2. 2 Timothy 3:14–17 — Look for what Scripture is useful for.
  3. Luke 24:25–27 — Notice how Jesus helps His followers understand how Scripture points toward God’s plan.

1

The Bible is a library, not one giant chapter.

The Bible contains many books written across a long period of history. It includes history, songs, wisdom, prophecy, letters, biographies of Jesus called Gospels, and more.

That means we should ask good questions: Who is speaking? Who are they speaking to? What happened before and after this verse? What kind of writing is this? What does the passage teach about God, people, sin, wisdom, faith, or salvation?

Reading one sentence without its surrounding story can sometimes lead us to misunderstand it.

2

Scripture helps us know God’s story.

The Bible is not mainly a collection of random inspirational sayings. It tells one big story: God created the world, people rebelled against Him, God continued His rescue plan, Jesus came as Savior and King, and God is making all things new.

When you read a Bible story, ask more than, “Which character am I?” Ask, “What is God doing in this story?”

David and Goliath can teach courage, but the story is also about God’s faithfulness to His people. Daniel teaches courage too, but the point is not that every brave person will always escape danger.

3

The Bible teaches, corrects, and trains us.

Second Timothy says Scripture is useful for teaching, correcting, and training in righteousness. Sometimes the Bible encourages us. Sometimes it challenges us. Sometimes it shows us that something we believed or did was wrong.

Correction is not humiliation.

God’s truth leads us toward repentance and growth. A trusted parent, teacher, pastor, or leader should use Scripture with care—not as a weapon to frighten, control, or embarrass children.

4

Understanding grows with practice.

You will not understand every verse the first time you read it. Adults do not understand everything immediately either.

When something is confusing, read the verses before and after it, compare another Bible translation with a trusted adult, use a reliable study Bible, ask a mature Christian, and keep reading instead of giving up.

Questions can be part of learning.

5

Knowing Scripture and living Scripture belong together.

Memorizing verses and knowing Bible facts are valuable, but God’s Word is meant to shape how we live.

If we read that God cares about truth, we should practice honesty. If Jesus welcomes people who are overlooked, we should notice others. If Scripture teaches forgiveness, generosity, courage, or humility, we should ask how those truths change our choices.

Let’s Practice

Observe, understand, live it.

Choose a short passage such as Mark 10:13–16.

1.

Observe: What actually happens? Who is there? What words or actions stand out?

2.

Understand: What does this show us about Jesus? What was happening in the story?

3.

Live it: What is one faithful response? Maybe remembering that children matter to Jesus or treating younger children with patience and dignity.

Talk About It

Questions for a child and trusted adult.

  1. Why can reading only one verse sometimes be confusing?
  2. Name two different kinds of writing found in the Bible.
  3. According to 2 Timothy 3, what is Scripture useful for?
  4. Is it wrong to have questions about a Bible passage? Why or why not?
  5. Who are trustworthy people you can ask when you do not understand something?
  6. What is the difference between knowing a Bible fact and living out biblical truth?
  7. What Bible story or book would you like to understand better?

Try It This Week

Use a five-day Gospel rhythm.

  • Day 1: Read it slowly.
  • Day 2: Notice repeated words or important actions.
  • Day 3: Ask what it teaches about God or Jesus.
  • Day 4: Ask what it shows about people.
  • Day 5: Choose one way to respond in prayer or action.

You do not need to write anything online or share personal information. You may keep notes privately on paper or in a Bible notebook.

Memory Verse

Psalm 119:105

Learn the verse from the translation your family, church, or class uses. Talk about the picture of God’s Word as light for a path.

Prayer

Ask God for wisdom.

God, thank You for giving us Your Word. Help me read carefully, ask good questions, and understand what You are teaching. Give me wisdom when something is difficult. Help me not only know Bible words but live in a way that honors You. Show me Jesus as I read Scripture and help me grow in truth and love. Amen.

Parent / Teacher Note

Build trust in Scripture and healthy reading habits together.

Main objective: Give children a healthy approach to Scripture that combines trust in the Bible with basic reading-in-context habits. Avoid teaching that every verse is a private message directly about them or that every Bible story promises the same outcome in their lives.

Encourage questions. A child who says, “I do not understand this” is engaging with the text. Model responsible answers and use reliable resources when needed.

Safeguarding

Never use Scripture to pressure a child into secrecy, unquestioning obedience to unsafe adult behavior, or silence about harm. Biblical authority does not cancel appropriate safeguarding. If a child reports abuse, danger, or threatening behavior, follow safeguarding procedures and applicable reporting requirements.

This study does not request or collect personal information from children.

Suggested closing question: What is one question you can ask the next time you read a Bible passage?
