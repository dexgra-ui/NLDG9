Preteen & Teen Bible Studies · Lesson 3 of 10

How to Read Scripture Without Pretending.

You do not need to pretend every passage is easy. Faithful Bible reading means slowing down, noticing context, asking good questions, and staying teachable.

📖 2 Timothy 3:14–17Acts 17:10–12Luke 24:25–2735–50 minutesAges 11–18

Big Question

How do I read the Bible when I do not understand everything right away?

Big Truth

Faithful Bible reading does not require pretending every passage is easy.

God invites us to read carefully, ask good questions, pay attention to context, compare Scripture with Scripture, and remain teachable. Understanding grows over time.

Open Your Bible

Read, notice, and examine.

  1. 2 Timothy 3:14–17 — Notice what Scripture is useful for and what kind of life it is meant to form.
  2. Acts 17:10–12 — The Bereans listened eagerly and examined Scripture to see whether what they heard was true.
  3. Luke 24:25–27 — Watch how Jesus helps His followers understand the larger story of Scripture.

1

The Bible is not a collection of random inspirational sentences.

Every verse belongs to a paragraph, every paragraph belongs to a book, and every biblical book belongs to the larger story of God’s work in creation, Israel, Jesus, the church, and the promised renewal of all things.

Pulling one sentence out of context can make Scripture appear to say something the writer never meant.

2

Good questions are part of careful reading.

Ask: Who is speaking or writing? Who is being addressed? What happened before and after? What kind of writing is this? What does the passage actually say before I ask what it means for me? Does my interpretation fit the rest of Scripture?

Questions like these are not a sign of weak faith. They are part of taking Scripture seriously.

3

Read to be formed, not merely to collect facts.

Second Timothy says Scripture teaches, corrects, and trains. Bible knowledge matters, but the goal is not winning trivia contests or arguments. Scripture exposes our thinking, reveals God’s character, points us to Christ, and trains us for faithful living.

4

Do not confuse Scripture with every opinion about Scripture.

Study Bibles, sermons, videos, podcasts, devotionals, commentaries, teachers, and even AI tools can be helpful. They are not the Bible itself.

A wise reader can say, “The passage says this. My teacher suggests this. My denomination interprets this part this way. Other faithful Christians may understand this secondary question differently.”

5

Some passages take time.

You will encounter difficult history, unfamiliar customs, poetry, laws, symbolism, and passages Christians interpret differently. Read the surrounding chapters, use trustworthy resources, ask mature believers, and keep studying.

Think It Through

What would you check before saying, “This verse proves my point”?

Look at the surrounding passage, the speaker and audience, the kind of writing, and the argument of the biblical book. A true sentence can still be used misleadingly when it is pulled out of its context.

When a passage is confusing or uncomfortable, pretending to understand it is not maturity. Slowing down and studying carefully is.

Talk About It

Questions for discussion.

  1. What does 2 Timothy 3 say Scripture is meant to do?
  2. What do you notice about the Bereans in Acts 17?
  3. Why can a true sentence become misleading when removed from context?
  4. How can knowing the type of biblical writing help interpretation?
  5. Why should we distinguish Scripture from sermons, devotionals, social posts, or AI answers?
  6. What should you do when faithful Christians interpret a secondary issue differently?
  7. What Bible-reading habit would you like to strengthen?

You may ask questions without being mocked. You may also pass on a personal question; careful Bible study does not require private disclosure.

Preteen Track · roughly 11–14

LOOK · LEARN · LIVE

  1. LOOK — What does the passage actually say?
  2. LEARN — What does it teach about God, people, sin, grace, wisdom, or obedience?
  3. LIVE — What faithful response might it call for?

Practice with Mark 2:1–12 or Luke 10:25–37.

Teen Deeper Dive · roughly 14–18

Test a verse you have seen online.

Read at least ten verses before and after it, then ask whether the popular use matches the original context.

Go deeper: Interpretation asks what a text means in context. Application asks how that truth should shape life today.

Try It This Week

Read one complete chapter each day.

Choose a Gospel or New Testament letter. Before reaching for a commentary or video, write one observation, one question, one truth about God or discipleship, and one possible application. Then compare your observations with a trustworthy resource or trusted Christian leader.

Memory Verse

2 Timothy 3:16–17

Learn the passage from the Bible translation your family, church, or class uses.

Prayer

Read with humility.

God, thank You for giving us Scripture. Help me read with humility instead of rushing to make the Bible say what I already want. Give me patience with difficult passages, wisdom to recognize context, and courage to change when Your Word corrects me. Help me listen to teachers without treating human opinions as equal to Scripture. Lead me toward Jesus and faithful obedience. Amen.

Leader / Parent Note

Model careful interpretation rather than false certainty.

Purpose: Teach basic biblical interpretation without overwhelming students with technical language. Emphasize careful reading, context, humility, and lifelong growth.

Secondary issues

Explain your church’s view where appropriate, but identify interpretation as interpretation rather than pretending every disputed conclusion is stated directly in the text. Represent faithful Christian differences fairly.

Source discernment

Encourage students to verify claims rather than treating viral clips, AI answers, screenshots, or isolated quotations as reliable Bible teaching.

Dignity and safety

Do not ridicule misunderstanding or force personal disclosure. If a serious safety concern emerges in discussion, follow safeguarding procedures and applicable reporting requirements. Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep.

This study does not ask young people to submit personal information, private stories, names, ages, schools, locations, photos, or contact details.
