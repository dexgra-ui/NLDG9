Following Jesus Every Day.

Following Jesus is not only something we do at church. Discipleship reaches home, school, friendships, technology, service, rest, and the choices nobody else sees.

📖 Matthew 22:37–39Luke 9:23Colossians 2:6–7James 1:22Micah 6:820–30 minutesAges 7–11

Big Question

What does it look like to follow Jesus in ordinary everyday life?

Big Truth

Following Jesus is a whole-life journey.

Because Jesus has loved and saved us, we learn to trust and obey Him in ordinary life—at home, at school, in friendships, online, at church, and when nobody is watching. Discipleship is a lifelong journey of grace, truth, love, repentance, and growth.

Open Your Bible

Read and notice.

  1. Matthew 22:37–39 — Notice the two great commands Jesus gives.
  2. Colossians 2:6–7 — Look for the picture of being rooted and built up in Christ.
  3. James 1:22 — Notice the difference between hearing God’s Word and doing what it says.

1

Following Jesus reaches every part of life.

It can be easy to think faith belongs mainly to Sunday mornings, Bible studies, or prayer time. But Jesus is Lord of our whole lives.

Following Him shapes the way we speak to family members, treat classmates, use technology, respond when we lose, handle money, tell the truth, work at school, rest, apologize, choose friends, and care for people who need help.

There is no part of life where Jesus says, “That part does not matter to Me.”

2

Love God and love people.

Jesus summarized God’s commands by telling us to love God with our whole selves and love our neighbors as ourselves. These commands belong together.

We cannot say we love God while choosing cruelty toward people. And Christian love for people grows from knowing God, not simply from trying to be pleasant.

Everyday question:

How can I honor God and love the person in front of me?

3

Small choices form habits.

Most discipleship happens through ordinary repeated choices: one honest answer, one prayer, one apology, one decision not to gossip, one moment of patience, one Bible passage read carefully, one act of generosity, one decision to ask for help.

Small choices do not earn God’s love, but they can become habits that help us walk faithfully.

4

Following Jesus includes rest and limits.

Serving God does not mean doing everything. Jesus rested. He withdrew to pray. He did not meet every demand from every person at every moment.

Children especially should not be taught that being a “good Christian” means carrying adult problems, always saying yes, never feeling tired, or sacrificing healthy boundaries.

Faithful living includes sleep, play, learning, family time, worship, friendship, and receiving care from others.

5

We need other believers.

Following Jesus is personal, but it is not meant to be lonely. God gives us families, churches, mentors, teachers, and Christian friends who can encourage us, teach Scripture, pray with us, and correct us when needed.

No human leader is perfect. We follow Jesus first. Healthy Christian leaders point people toward Christ rather than trying to control them.

6

Growth is not the same as perfection.

A child may finish these ten studies and still struggle with fear, anger, honesty, prayer, comparison, forgiveness, or many other things. That does not mean the studies failed.

Growing with Jesus takes a lifetime.

When you fall, return to Him. When you do not understand, keep learning. When you sin, confess. When you are tired, receive rest. When someone needs love, ask how you can serve wisely. When you are afraid, remember God’s presence and seek help when needed.

A Simple Daily Rhythm

A picture of a growing life, not a scorecard.

L

Listen — Read or remember a small part of God’s Word.

P

Pray — Talk honestly with God.

L

Love — Look for one person to treat with Christlike dignity.

O

Obey — Take the next step you know is right.

G

Gather — Stay connected to healthy Christian community.

S

Serve — Use what God gives you to help others.

R

Rest — Receive limits and rest as gifts, not failures.

You do not need to complete every item perfectly every day. This is a picture of a growing life, not a scorecard.

Let’s Think About It

Faith belongs in the ordinary moments.

You are kind at church but cruel in a group chat where adults cannot see. That is not following Jesus every day. Jesus cares about hidden choices too.

You are exhausted and someone asks you to take on another responsibility that should belong to an adult. Following Jesus does not always mean saying yes. Children need appropriate boundaries.

You make the same mistake again after asking God to help you change. Do not give up. Tell the truth, receive correction and grace, use wise support, and keep growing.

Talk About It

Questions for a child and trusted adult.

  1. What do the two great commandments teach us about everyday faith?
  2. Where is it easiest for you to remember Jesus? Where is it harder?
  3. How can small choices turn into habits?
  4. Why are rest and healthy limits part of faithful living?
  5. Why do Christians need community?
  6. What should we do when we fail again?
  7. Which part of the daily rhythm—listen, pray, love, obey, gather, serve, rest—would help you most right now?

Try It This Week

Choose three parts of the Daily Rhythm.

Practice them for one week. For example:

  • Listen — Read one short Gospel paragraph each morning.
  • Pray — Thank God for one thing and ask for one thing.
  • Love — Do one thoughtful action for someone each day.

Or choose a different three that fit your season. At the end of the week, do not grade yourself. Ask: “What helped me pay attention to Jesus? What would I like to continue?”

Memory Verse

Colossians 2:6–7

Learn these verses from the Bible translation your family, church, or class uses. Notice the pictures of walking, being rooted, being built up, and growing in thankfulness.

Prayer

Keep growing with Jesus.

Jesus, thank You for calling me to follow You. Help my faith be real at church, at home, at school, with friends, online, and when nobody else sees. Teach me to love God and love people. Help me listen to Your Word, pray honestly, obey what is right, serve with humility, and receive healthy rest. When I fail, help me return to You and keep growing. Amen.

Parent / Teacher Note

End the journey with grace, not a behavior scorecard.

Main objective: Help children see discipleship as whole-life formation rooted in grace rather than a checklist for earning approval.

Do not turn the Daily Rhythm into a behavior scorecard. The practices are tools for relationship and growth, not a way to measure which child is “more spiritual.” Make room for different learning styles, family situations, disabilities, schedules, and levels of maturity.

Emphasize that children should remain children. They are not responsible for fixing adult relationships, counseling peers through crises, carrying ministry workloads, or keeping adults emotionally stable. Service should be age-appropriate and safely supervised.

Healthy church community includes accountability for adults too. No leader should demand secrecy, private access, inappropriate physical contact, or unquestioning loyalty. If a child raises a safety concern, follow safeguarding procedures and applicable reporting requirements. Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep.

This study does not create child accounts, collect personal information, or require children to publish their reflections.

Suggested closing question: What is one way you want to keep growing with Jesus after this study ends?
