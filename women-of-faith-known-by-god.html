Known by God

God knows every part of our lives, remains present with us, and created us with dignity and purpose.

📖 Psalm 139:1–18Supporting: Luke 12:6–7; Ephesians 2:1045–60 minutesIdentity and belonging

Central biblical truth

God knows us completely, remains present with us, and created us with dignity and purpose. Our deepest identity is received from Him rather than earned through roles, approval, appearance, achievement, or constant productivity.

Study introduction

Many women spend years learning how to meet expectations.

We learn what people want from us, what responsibilities must be handled, how strong we are supposed to appear, and which parts of our story are safer to hide. We may become known for what we do while feeling that no one truly knows who we are.

Psalm 139 begins with a deeply personal truth: God knows us.

He knows our actions, thoughts, words, routines, fears, strengths, wounds, and unfinished growth. His knowledge is not distant observation. He is present with us.

For someone carrying shame, being fully known may sound frightening. We may assume that if people knew everything, they would reject us. But David’s response is not terror. It is wonder. God’s complete knowledge is joined with His presence, creative care, and invitation into an honest relationship.

Scripture focus

Read Psalm 139:1–18 slowly. As you read, notice what the passage says about:

  • What God knows
  • Where God is present
  • How God formed human life
  • How the writer responds

Read the passage a second time and mark any words or ideas that repeat.

1. God knows you completely

“You have searched me, Lord, and you know me.”Psalm 139:1

The Psalm does not say God only knows our public behavior. He knows when we sit and rise. He understands our thoughts. He is familiar with our ways. He knows our words before we speak them.

Nothing about us surprises God.

This does not mean God approves of everything we think or do. It means we cannot build a relationship with Him through pretending.

God already knows the truth. That makes confession possible. We are not informing God of something He missed. We are agreeing with Him, bringing what is hidden into the light, and receiving His grace.

Consider

  • What parts of your life are easiest to show others?
  • What parts do you work hardest to hide?

2. God is present in every season

“Where can I go from your Spirit? Where can I flee from your presence?”Psalm 139:7

David imagines the highest place, the deepest place, the farthest distance, and the darkest night. God is present in all of them.

Some seasons make God’s presence easier to recognize. Others feel silent, confusing, lonely, or painful. Psalm 139 does not promise that every place will feel safe or pleasant. It promises that no place can remove us from God’s reach.

Even darkness is not dark to Him.

God can meet women in joy, grief, marriage, singleness, motherhood, infertility, caregiving, career changes, illness, disappointment, leadership, loneliness, and new beginnings. No season gives you greater value than another. No difficult season makes you invisible to God.

Consider

  • Which season of your life has made God feel especially near?
  • Which season has made His presence harder to recognize?

3. God formed you with dignity

“For you created my inmost being; you knit me together in my mother’s womb.”Psalm 139:13

Human dignity begins with God.

The Psalm describes intentional, personal, creative care. David is amazed that God was involved before anyone else saw or understood his life.

This truth confronts the labels women often receive from appearance, comparison, rejection, age, disability, fertility, relationship status, education, income, or past mistakes.

Your worth is not decided by who approves of you. It is not measured by whether you match someone else’s idea of beauty, success, femininity, marriage, motherhood, leadership, or ministry. You bear God-given dignity because you were created by Him.

Important distinction

Psalm 139 does not teach that every painful experience was good or personally designed by God. It teaches that human life is known and valued by Him. We should not use this passage to dismiss abuse, illness, grief, injustice, or trauma with shallow explanations.

4. God’s purpose is received, not earned

“For we are God’s handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works.”Ephesians 2:10

Purpose is not the same as constant productivity.

Women can feel pressure to prove their worth by doing more, carrying more, serving more, and needing less. But good works flow from grace. They do not purchase God’s love.

God’s purpose may be expressed through work, family, ministry, friendship, creativity, prayer, service, leadership, hospitality, advocacy, generosity, or quiet faithfulness. Different seasons allow for different capacities.

Rest, healing, receiving help, setting boundaries, and caring for health can also be faithful responses to God.

Consider

Where have you confused being useful with being valuable?

Identity before roles

Roles can be meaningful gifts, but they cannot carry the full weight of identity.

A woman may be married or single, a mother or not a mother, employed inside or outside the home, young or older, healthy or living with limitations, publicly visible or serving quietly, leading or learning, certain or still asking questions.

None of these determines whether she is fully seen and loved by God.

In Christ, identity begins with belonging to Him. Roles may change. Seasons may change. People’s opinions may change. God’s knowledge and presence remain.

Conversation questions

  1. Which verse or phrase from Psalm 139 stood out to you most?
  2. Does being fully known by God feel comforting, frightening, or both?
  3. Which labels have most influenced how you see yourself?
  4. Have you ever measured your worth by a role, relationship, achievement, or appearance?
  5. What does Psalm 139 reveal about God’s presence in difficult seasons?
  6. How can confession become an act of trust rather than shame?
  7. What is the difference between God-given purpose and pressure to remain constantly productive?
  8. Where might God be inviting you to receive His truth more deeply?

Pause and consider

Complete these statements privately. Your answers do not need to be shared unless you choose to share them with someone trustworthy.

Live it this week

Practice 1: Read Psalm 139 daily

  • Day 1: verses 1–6
  • Day 2: verses 7–12
  • Day 3: verses 13–18
  • Day 4: read the entire Psalm
  • Day 5: write what the passage reveals about God
  • Day 6: write what the passage reveals about human dignity
  • Day 7: pray through the passage

Practice 2: Replace one label with truth

Write down one label that has shaped your identity. Then write a Scripture-shaped truth beside it.

Example: “I am invisible” becomes “God has searched me, knows me, and remains present with me.”

Practice 3: Allow yourself to be known safely

Share one honest thought, question, or need with a trustworthy woman, mentor, pastor, counselor, or mature believer. Being known does not require disclosing private information to everyone. Wisdom and healthy boundaries still matter.

Prayer

Father, thank You for knowing me completely. You see what others notice and what they overlook. You know my strengths, fears, wounds, questions, and unfinished growth.

Forgive me for allowing labels, roles, comparison, rejection, or achievement to define my worth.

Help me receive my identity from You. Teach me to live honestly in Your presence, accept Your grace, and walk in the purpose You provide without trying to earn Your love.

Give me wisdom to build healthy relationships where truth and grace are practiced. Help me see other women with the dignity You have given them.

In Jesus’ name, amen.

Leader and mentor notes

Main objective

Help women ground their identity in God’s knowledge, presence, and creative care rather than in roles, appearance, approval, achievement, or pain.

Keep the discussion Scripture-centered

Allow women to share experiences, but regularly return to what Psalm 139 actually says. Avoid turning the session into general encouragement without biblical grounding.

Do not assume every woman has the same life goals

Do not imply that marriage, motherhood, career, public ministry, or a particular personality represents God’s plan for every woman.

Listen for

  • Shame and fear of being fully known
  • Identity built entirely around caregiving or productivity
  • Pain related to rejection, infertility, singleness, divorce, widowhood, aging, disability, appearance, or family expectations
  • Spiritual manipulation involving submission, silence, loyalty, or gender expectations
  • Trauma or abuse requiring qualified support
  • Exhaustion caused by carrying responsibilities alone

Avoid

  • Pressuring women to disclose private experiences
  • Suggesting all suffering was directly sent to teach a lesson
  • Using Psalm 139 to dismiss grief, trauma, or injustice
  • Treating appearance or personality as evidence of spiritual maturity
  • Encouraging reconciliation without safety, accountability, or changed behavior
  • Offering counseling, medical, or legal conclusions beyond your role

Suggested closing question

What truth from this study do you need another woman to remind you of this week?
