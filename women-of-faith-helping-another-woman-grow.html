Helping Another Woman Grow

Mature disciples listen, pray, open Scripture, share wisdom humbly, model faithfulness, and help another woman follow Jesus without creating dependence.

📖 2 Timothy 1:3–7; 2 Timothy 2:2Supporting: Matthew 28:18–20; Titus 2:3–545–60 minutesMentoring, multiplication, and continued growth

Central biblical truth

Every growing disciple can help someone else follow Jesus. Christian mentoring points to Christ and Scripture, models faithful life, respects boundaries, admits limits, and prepares the other woman to grow in wisdom and help others.

Study introduction

Many women assume they need more knowledge, confidence, or perfection before they can encourage someone else.

Paul reminded Timothy of faith passed through generations and instructed him to entrust truth to faithful people who could teach others.

Mentoring is not having every answer. It is walking beside another woman with Scripture, prayer, honesty, wisdom, accountability, and care.

Scripture focus

Read 2 Timothy 1:3–7, 2 Timothy 2:2, and Matthew 28:18–20. Notice:

  • Faith passed through relationships
  • Gifts that need encouragement
  • Truth entrusted rather than possessed
  • The expectation that disciples make disciples
  • Christ’s presence in the work

1. Your faith has a history

Timothy’s faith was connected to Lois and Eunice. Their influence mattered even though Scripture gives few details about their daily lives.

Women often shape faith through prayer, conversation, courage, hospitality, teaching, correction, endurance, and example.

Think about the women whose faith helped you see Jesus more clearly. Their influence can become part of what you pass forward.

2. Mentoring begins with presence and listening

A mentor should learn the woman’s actual story rather than forcing her into a prepared script.

Good questions often serve better than quick advice. Listening reveals what she understands, fears, hopes, and needs.

Presence builds trust, but healthy mentoring also keeps appropriate time, communication, and emotional boundaries.

3. Open Scripture together

The mentor’s opinion is not the final authority. Read passages in context, distinguish clear biblical teaching from personal interpretation, and admit where Christians differ.

Help the other woman observe the text, ask questions, apply truth, and grow in her own ability to study Scripture.

The goal is not permanent dependence on the mentor. It is increasing dependence on Christ and maturity in the community of faith.

4. Know your role and limits

A mentor is not a therapist, physician, attorney, emergency responder, pastor, or spiritual ruler unless she separately holds those roles with proper training and accountability.

Mentors should refer concerns beyond their competence and involve appropriate leaders when safety or serious harm is involved.

Humility protects both women.

5. Discipleship should multiply

Second Timothy 2:2 describes truth moving from Paul to Timothy to faithful people and then to others.

A healthy mentor celebrates when the other woman grows, develops discernment, serves, and begins encouraging someone else.

Completion is not graduation from Christian growth. It is a transition into continued discipleship, service, and community.

A simple mentoring rhythm

  1. Check in honestly
  2. Read and discuss Scripture
  3. Ask what God is revealing
  4. Identify one practical response
  5. Pray together
  6. Follow up without controlling

Conversation questions

  1. Which women have influenced your faith?
  2. What makes someone feel safe to learn with?
  3. Why is listening essential in mentoring?
  4. How can a mentor keep Scripture central?
  5. What are signs of unhealthy dependence?
  6. Which concerns require referral or added support?
  7. What can you share now without pretending to know everything?
  8. Who might God be inviting you to encourage?

Pause and consider

Live it this week

Practice 1: Honor a spiritual influence

Thank a woman who helped shape your faith, whether publicly or quietly.

Practice 2: Invite one conversation

Ask a woman whether she would like to read Scripture, pray, or talk regularly for a season.

Practice 3: Build a healthy plan

Agree on purpose, frequency, confidentiality limits, communication expectations, and when pastoral or professional support should be involved.

Prayer

Jesus, thank You for the people who helped me grow. Make me faithful with what I have received.

Give me humility to listen, wisdom to open Scripture carefully, courage to speak truth, and love that respects dignity and boundaries. Keep me from control, pride, and pretending to have every answer.

Help women grow into mature disciples who strengthen others and serve Your church. In Your name, amen.

Leader and mentor notes

Main objective

Prepare women to begin healthy, Scripture-centered mentoring relationships that encourage maturity and multiplication.

Ministry safeguards

Churches should use screening, training, supervision, matching guidance, confidentiality standards, reporting procedures, and clear referral pathways, especially when minors or vulnerable adults are involved.

Avoid

  • Pairing women without considering safety and capacity
  • Creating secretive or exclusive relationships
  • Allowing mentors to act beyond competence
  • Demanding obedience to personal preferences
  • Measuring success by emotional dependence
  • Ending contact abruptly after the study finishes

Suggested closing question

What have you received from God that you can faithfully pass to another woman?
