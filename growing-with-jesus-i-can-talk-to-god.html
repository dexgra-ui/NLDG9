I Can Talk to God.

Prayer is not a performance or a magic formula. It is honest relationship with a wise and loving God.

📖 Matthew 6:5–13Psalm 62:8Philippians 4:6–7Mark 14:32–3620–30 minutesAges 7–11

Big Question

What is prayer, and how can I talk to God?

Big Truth

Prayer is talking with God.

We can worship Him, thank Him, confess sin, ask for help, and tell Him honestly what we are feeling. Prayer is not a performance or a magic formula. God is wise and loving, and we can trust Him even when His answer is not what we expected.

Open Your Bible

Read and notice.

  1. Matthew 6:5–13 — Jesus teaches His followers how to pray and warns them not to pray just to impress people.
  2. Psalm 62:8 — Notice the invitation to pour out our hearts before God.
  3. Mark 14:32–36 — Jesus Himself prays honestly in a very difficult moment.

1

Prayer is relationship, not a show.

Jesus taught that prayer should not be about trying to sound impressive. God does not need fancy words.

You can pray with a few words or many words. You can pray quietly, out loud, alone, with your family, or with other believers. You can pray when you are happy, confused, thankful, angry, afraid, or sorry.

God already knows what is happening, but prayer helps us bring our hearts to Him in trust.

2

Jesus gave us a pattern for prayer.

In the Lord’s Prayer, Jesus teaches us to begin with God—His name, His kingdom, and His will. Then He teaches us to ask for what we need, seek forgiveness, forgive others, and ask for help against temptation and evil.

This is a pattern, not a password. God does not refuse to listen because you forgot the exact order.

P

Praise — Tell God what is true about Him.

S

Sorry — Confess where you have sinned.

T

Thanks — Thank God for His gifts and faithfulness.

H

Help — Ask for what you or others need.

You do not have to use this every time. It is simply a tool.

3

You can be honest with God.

Psalm 62 tells God’s people to pour out their hearts before Him. The Psalms include joy, praise, confusion, grief, fear, frustration, and hope. God is not surprised by your feelings.

Honesty with God does not mean every feeling should control our actions. We can tell God, “I am really angry,” and still ask Him to help us choose what is right.

4

God is not a vending machine.

Sometimes people imagine prayer like putting in the right words and getting whatever they want. But God is not a machine we control.

Jesus prayed in Gethsemane and asked the Father if the suffering ahead could pass from Him. He also prayed for the Father’s will to be done.

God may answer prayer in ways we understand quickly, in ways we understand later, or in ways we do not understand in this life. Sometimes the answer may be yes, no, wait, or something different from what we imagined.

Remember:

Prayer is trust, not control.

5

Prayer and wise action can go together.

  • If you are worried about a test, pray—and study.
  • If you hurt someone, pray—and apologize.
  • If you are being bullied or feel unsafe, pray—and tell a trusted adult who can help.
  • If someone is sick, pray—and let adults seek appropriate medical care.

Faith does not mean refusing help. God often works through wise people, families, churches, doctors, counselors, teachers, and other helpers.

Let’s Think About It

Prayer is bigger than getting the result we wanted.

You prayed to win a game, but your team lost. Did God fail to hear you?

No. Prayer is not a guarantee that every event will go the way we want. You can still thank God, learn, show good character, and trust Him.

You are scared about something happening at school. Should you only pray and keep it secret?

No. Prayer is good, and telling a trustworthy adult can also be the right thing to do.

Talk About It

Questions for a child and trusted adult.

  1. What does Jesus warn against in Matthew 6?
  2. Why do you think God wants us to pray if He already knows what we need?
  3. What feelings can we bring to God?
  4. Why is prayer not a magic formula?
  5. What can we learn from Jesus’ prayer in Gethsemane?
  6. Name a situation where prayer and wise action belong together.
  7. Which part of prayer—praise, sorry, thanks, or help—is easiest for you? Which is harder?

Try It This Week

Try a five-minute prayer rhythm.

  • Minute 1: Be quiet and remember that God is with you.
  • Minute 2: Praise God for who He is.
  • Minute 3: Thank Him for something specific.
  • Minute 4: Ask for help for yourself or someone else.
  • Minute 5: Ask God to help you follow His will.

You can shorten or change this rhythm. The goal is relationship, not completing a perfect routine.

Memory Verse

Philippians 4:6–7

Learn these verses from the Bible translation your family, church, or class uses. Notice that prayer and thanksgiving are connected with bringing our requests to God.

Prayer

Talk honestly with God.

Father, thank You that I can talk to You. Help me be honest instead of trying to sound impressive. Forgive my sin and help me forgive others. Thank You for the good gifts You give. Please help me with the things I am carrying today. Give me wisdom to act when I need to act and trust You when I do not understand the answer. In Jesus’ name, amen.

Parent / Teacher Note

Teach prayer without placing spiritual blame on children.

Main objective: Teach prayer as relationship with God rather than performance, superstition, or a guaranteed way to control outcomes.

Avoid telling children that unanswered prayer means they lacked faith. Do not suggest that illness, family conflict, abuse, financial hardship, or other serious problems continue because a child did not pray correctly. That can create harmful guilt.

Make room for lament and disappointment. Children can learn to say, “I asked God for this, and it did not happen. I am sad and I still want to trust Him.”

Safeguarding

If prayer discussion leads a child to disclose danger, abuse, self-harm concerns, or another serious safety issue, listen calmly and follow safeguarding procedures and applicable reporting requirements. Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep.

This study does not ask children to submit prayer requests or personal information online.

Suggested closing question: What is one honest sentence you could say to God today?
