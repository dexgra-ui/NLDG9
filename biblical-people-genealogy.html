Accuracy First

A family tree is only as good as its labels.

Some biblical names clearly identify individual people. Others function as ancestor names, nations, or clans. Some relationships are explicit; a few are probable from immediate context; manuscript traditions sometimes preserve variant forms. This reference keeps those categories visible.

Scripture-stated

A parent, spouse, child, sibling, clan, or other connection is recorded as explicit only when the biblical text states it.

Probable, not promoted to fact

When a relationship is strongly suggested but not directly worded, the record says probable instead of pretending the inference is a quotation from Scripture.

Textual variants stay visible

Names such as Dodanim/Rodanim are not “fixed” by choosing one spelling and hiding the manuscript issue. Variant forms remain part of the reference.

This becomes the whole-Bible reference one verified section at a time.

Phase 2 · Genesis 12–50

Abraham, Sarah, Hagar, Ishmael, Isaac, Rebekah, Esau, Jacob, the twelve sons, and the extended patriarchal families.

Phase 3 · Exodus–Deuteronomy

Moses’ generation, Aaronic and Levitical lines, tribal leaders, elders, and named wilderness figures.

Later phases

Historical books, Chronicles genealogies, kings, prophets, exile and return, Gospels, Acts, and every named person in the Epistles and Revelation.
