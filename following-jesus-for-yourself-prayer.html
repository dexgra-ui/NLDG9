Prayer When You Don’t Know What to Say.

Prayer is not a performance. You can come to God honestly when you are grateful, distracted, angry, confused, doubtful, quiet, or unsure what words to use.

📖 Matthew 6:5–13Psalm 13Philippians 4:4–735–50 minutesAges 11–18

Big Question

How do I pray when I feel awkward, distracted, upset, doubtful, or unsure what to say?

Big Truth

Prayer is not a performance.

Jesus teaches His followers to come to God honestly, simply, and with trust. Prayer can include praise, questions, confession, requests, silence, gratitude, and grief. God is not impressed by fancy words; He invites truthful relationship.

Open Your Bible

Notice what biblical prayer actually sounds like.

  1. Matthew 6:5–13 — Notice what Jesus warns against before He gives a model prayer.
  2. Psalm 13 — Watch the movement from “How long?” toward trust without pretending the pain disappeared instantly.
  3. Philippians 4:4–7 — Notice the connection between prayer, thanksgiving, and God’s peace.

1

Prayer is not about sounding spiritual.

Jesus warns against praying to impress people. Prayer is not a contest for who can sound the most mature, emotional, poetic, or confident.

A simple “God, I need help” can be more honest than a long prayer filled with words you do not mean.

2

Honest prayer can include difficult emotions.

Psalm 13 is not fake positivity. The writer feels forgotten and overwhelmed, and he says so. God already knows what you feel.

The goal is not to turn every emotion into truth, but to bring every emotion into God’s presence where it can be examined by truth.

3

The Lord’s Prayer gives prayer a healthy shape.

Jesus teaches us to pray about God’s name and kingdom, daily needs, forgiveness, relationships, temptation, and dependence.

A simple guide:

HONOR — God, You are holy. · SURRENDER — Let Your will shape mine. · ASK — Give what is needed today. · CONFESS — Forgive me and help me forgive. · PROTECT — Lead me away from temptation and evil.

4

Prayer does not guarantee instant answers.

Sometimes God answers quickly. Sometimes circumstances take time. Sometimes the answer is different from what we hoped. Prayer is not a technique for controlling God.

Christian trust asks boldly and honestly while still trusting God’s wisdom when we cannot see the whole picture.

5

Distraction does not mean you are a failure.

Minds wander. Phones buzz. Stress interrupts. When you notice your attention drifting, return to prayer instead of giving up.

You can pray with Scripture, write a prayer, take a quiet walk, pray one sentence at a time, or use a short list of people and concerns.

Think It Through

Honesty is the beginning of prayer, not the end of wisdom.

One student uses impressive language because everyone is listening. Another quietly says, “God, I am angry and confused. Please help me trust You.” Which sounds closer to Jesus’ teaching in Matthew 6?

A person can be honest with God and still be wrong about what they are feeling or asking for. Honest prayer brings the real heart into a relationship where God’s truth can shape it.

Talk About It

Questions for discussion.

  1. What does Jesus warn about in Matthew 6?
  2. Which part of the Lord’s Prayer feels easiest? Which feels hardest?
  3. What does Psalm 13 teach about painful emotions?
  4. What is the difference between honest prayer and demanding that God agree with us?
  5. How can thanksgiving change the way we pray about stress?
  6. What helps when your mind wanders?
  7. Why can silence be a meaningful part of prayer?

No one has to pray aloud. You may pass, pray silently, or write privately.

Preteen Track · roughly 11–14

Five-finger prayer.

  1. Praise God for who He is.
  2. Thank Him for something specific.
  3. Ask forgiveness for something you know was wrong.
  4. Ask for help for yourself.
  5. Pray for someone else.

Keep it simple. One sentence for each is enough.

Teen Deeper Dive · roughly 14–18

Relationship, not transaction.

Read a lament Psalm such as Psalm 42, 77, or 88. Notice whether the writer always reaches a neat emotional ending.

A transaction says, “I did this, so God owes me that.” Relationship says, “I trust God enough to ask, listen, obey, wait, and keep walking with Him.”

Try It This Week

Set aside ten minutes on three days.

Put your phone out of reach if possible. Spend about two minutes reading a short Psalm, three minutes telling God honestly what is on your mind, three minutes praying for other people, and two minutes sitting quietly and ending with gratitude.

Do not judge the prayer by whether you felt emotional. Ask whether you showed up honestly before God.

Memory Verse

Philippians 4:6–7

Learn the passage from the Bible translation your family, church, or class uses.

Prayer

Come as you are.

Father, thank You that I do not have to impress You. Teach me to pray honestly and humbly. When I am confused, help me bring my questions to You. When I am grateful, help me say so. When I sin, help me confess instead of hiding. When I am worried, teach me to ask for help and trust Your wisdom. Shape my desires so that I want Your will more than I want control. In Jesus’ name, amen.

Leader / Parent Note

Make prayer accessible without reducing it to a formula.

Purpose: The Lord’s Prayer is a model that forms priorities, not a magic sequence that guarantees outcomes.

No forced public prayer

Do not require students to pray aloud. A young person’s reluctance to pray publicly should not be treated as spiritual failure.

Do not promise instant relief

Avoid promising that prayer will immediately remove anxiety, depression, illness, family conflict, grief, or other serious problems. Prayer is central to Christian life, and appropriate professional care or practical help should not be discouraged.

Safeguarding

If a student mentions self-harm, abuse, exploitation, or immediate danger, follow safeguarding procedures and applicable reporting requirements. Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep.

This study does not ask young people to submit personal information, prayer requests, names, ages, schools, locations, photos, or contact details.
