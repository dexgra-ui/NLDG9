Preteen & Teen Bible Studies · Lesson 5 of 10

Questions, Doubt, and Trust.

A question does not have to become an exit door from faith. Scripture shows people bringing uncertainty toward Jesus, investigating carefully, and growing in trust.

📖 Mark 9:14–29John 20:24–31Jude 20–2335–50 minutesAges 11–18

Big Question

What should I do when I have questions about God or when my faith feels uncertain?

Big Truth

Questions do not have to be enemies of faith.

Healthy faith does not pretend every question is easy. It seeks truth honestly, examines evidence carefully, stays connected to wise believers, and keeps turning toward Christ.

Open Your Bible

Watch how Scripture treats people who are uncertain.

  1. Mark 9:14–29 — Notice the father’s honest words: he believes and also recognizes where he needs help.
  2. John 20:24–31 — Watch how Jesus responds to Thomas and why John says he recorded these signs.
  3. Jude 20–23 — Look for both personal responsibility and compassion toward people who doubt.

1

Faith and questions can exist in the same person.

The father in Mark 9 does not pretend to have no uncertainty. He brings his mixed faith and struggle into the open.

When a hard issue appears, a fragile faith may assume, “If I have a question, everything must be false.” Scripture gives a better path: bring the question into the light.

2

Not every kind of doubt is the same.

Uncertainty can come from missing information, pain, disappointment, intellectual questions, guilt, pressure, hypocrisy we have witnessed, or resistance to something difficult.

It helps to ask: What exactly am I questioning, and why? A vague “I doubt everything” becomes easier to examine when turned into a specific question.

3

Christianity does not ask you to believe without thinking.

John says he recorded signs so readers could believe that Jesus is the Christ. Christianity makes claims about history, testimony, the identity of Jesus, His death, and His resurrection.

Faith is not the absence of evidence. It is trust based on who God is and what He has revealed, even while some questions remain unresolved.

4

Doubt should move us toward investigation, not isolation.

People often withdraw because they fear being judged. Jude tells believers to show mercy to those who doubt.

A healthy church should allow sincere questions without mockery. At the same time, questioning should not become an excuse to avoid every conclusion forever. Investigation should move toward truth.

5

Some questions remain difficult.

Faithful Christians have wrestled with suffering, evil, science, biblical interpretation, other religions, unanswered prayer, and difficult passages for centuries.

Maturity does not say, “There are no hard questions,” or “A hard question means there is no answer.” Learn to distinguish questions with strong answers, secondary questions with several Christian interpretations, and mysteries where knowledge remains limited.

Think It Through

The same question can come from different postures.

One person may ask because they genuinely want to understand. Another may ask only to embarrass someone and refuse to listen to any answer. The words can sound similar while the posture is different.

And when an adult answers a difficult question with “Do not ask that. Just believe,” what kind of problem might that create?

Talk About It

Questions for discussion.

  1. What do you notice about the father’s prayer in Mark 9?
  2. How did Jesus respond to Thomas?
  3. Why did John say he wrote his Gospel?
  4. What are some reasons people experience doubt?
  5. Why is it useful to turn vague doubt into a specific question?
  6. What makes a source trustworthy when researching faith?
  7. What does Jude teach about treating people who doubt?

You may use a general example instead of a personal question. No one has to disclose private grief, trauma, family conflict, or another sensitive experience.

Preteen Track · roughly 11–14

Build a research plan.

Choose a faith question you have heard someone ask; it does not need to be personal. Then:

  1. Read a relevant Bible passage.
  2. Ask a trusted Christian adult.
  3. Check a reliable study resource.
  4. Write what you learned and what questions remain.

The goal is to practice investigating rather than panicking.

Teen Deeper Dive · roughly 14–18

Compare serious sources.

Define one substantial question precisely, then compare at least two thoughtful sources instead of relying on a short social-media clip.

Ask what evidence is offered, whether opposing views are represented fairly, what assumptions are operating, and whether biblical texts are used in context.

Try It This Week

Give one question focused attention.

Write three headings: WHAT I KNOW, WHAT I AM UNSURE ABOUT, and WHAT I WILL INVESTIGATE NEXT. Pray before and after your study. Ask God for truth, not merely the answer you prefer.

Memory Verse

Mark 9:24

Learn the verse from the Bible translation your family, church, or class uses.

Prayer

Bring your questions toward Jesus.

Jesus, thank You that I do not have to hide my questions from You. Help me seek truth with humility, courage, and patience. Protect me from fear, pride, laziness, and the desire to believe only what is comfortable. Give me wise people to learn from. When I do not have every answer, help me keep walking toward You rather than away from You. Amen.

Leader / Parent Note

Make questions examinable, not fashionable or shameful.

Purpose: Give young people permission to think while keeping a clear expectation that honest investigation matters.

Do not fake certainty

It is healthy to say, “I do not know; let’s research that carefully.” Avoid weak arguments merely because they support the conclusion you want.

Respect across traditions

Where Christian traditions differ on secondary issues, represent the main views fairly and return to the biblical text. Do not turn a question into a denominational loyalty test.

Safety and care

A theological question may connect to grief, trauma, family conflict, bullying, or another personal experience. Do not force disclosure. If a safety concern emerges, follow safeguarding procedures and applicable reporting requirements. Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep.

This study does not ask young people to submit personal information, private questions, names, ages, schools, locations, photos, or contact details.
