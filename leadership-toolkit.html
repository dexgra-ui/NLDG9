1

Decision & Discernment Guide

Make the decision in the light, not in isolation.

Clarify the issue, gather truthful information, include affected voices, seek counsel, and plan review.

Related studiesStudy 4: Wisdom & DiscernmentStudy 8: Accountability
Use this before a major ministry decision. A strong process does not guarantee an easy outcome, but it reduces secrecy, haste, avoidable harm, and personality-driven leadership.
2

Team Covenant Builder

Agree on how the team will serve before pressure tests it.

A covenant names shared practices without pretending that every relationship or disagreement will be simple.

Related studiesStudy 3: Leading Like JesusStudy 6: Healthy Teams
Build this together. A leader should not privately write rules for everyone else. Invite honest discussion, revise unclear language, and review the covenant regularly.
3

Accountability & Safeguarding Plan

Put authority under systems strong enough to protect people.

Good intentions are not a substitute for oversight, transparent processes, qualified review, and safe reporting.

Related studiesStudy 2: CharacterStudy 8: Power & Boundaries
This is a planning aid, not legal advice. Follow denominational policy, insurer requirements, employment rules, child-protection law, financial controls, and qualified professional guidance.
4

Conflict Repair Worksheet

Seek truth and repair without confusing reconciliation with unsafe access.

Listen, distinguish facts from interpretations, take responsibility, and identify boundaries and follow-up.

Related studiesStudy 7: Conflict & ReconciliationStudy 8: Accountability
Do not use this worksheet to privately manage abuse, threats, criminal conduct, harassment, exploitation, or serious safeguarding concerns. Prioritize immediate safety and appropriate reporting.
5

Leader Development Plan

Develop people through formation, opportunity, feedback, and release.

Leadership development should strengthen character and capacity without using people as unpaid labor or copies of the current leader.

Related studiesStudy 6: Healthy TeamsStudy 10: Multiplying Leaders
Write this with the developing leader. Respect calling, pace, accessibility, family responsibilities, employment, health, and the freedom to say no.
6

Leadership Transition Plan

Transfer responsibility without abandoning people or preserving control.

Faithful transitions document what matters, communicate truthfully, transfer access safely, and honor both outgoing and incoming leaders.

Related studiesStudy 9: Change & WearinessStudy 10: Finishing Faithfully
Begin earlier than feels necessary. A transition plan should serve the ministry and people, not protect one person’s status or force a successor to imitate the past.

Use the right response

A worksheet cannot replace protection, reporting, or professional care.

Leadership tools are helpful only when leaders remain accountable and know the limits of their role.

  • Immediate danger requires emergency help and protective action.
  • Abuse, exploitation, credible threats, and criminal conduct require appropriate reporting and qualified review.
  • Forgiveness does not erase consequences, boundaries, or leadership disqualification.
  • Legal, financial, medical, employment, and mental-health matters require qualified expertise.
  • Do not retaliate against people who raise concerns in good faith.
