Biblical Maps • Joshua 13–21

Conquest & the Twelve Tribes

See the tribal inheritances described in Joshua while keeping two important ideas separate: the land allotted to each tribe and the land each tribe actually occupied at a given moment were not always the same.

Open Branded Canva Map ↗Back to Biblical Maps
Accuracy note: Joshua gives detailed boundary descriptions, but several ancient boundary markers are uncertain, and ancient territorial lines were not surveyed like modern state borders. These overlays are therefore teaching approximations built around the biblical descriptions and established geographic anchors, not claims of meter-by-meter precision.
Allotment is not the same as occupation. Joshua and Judges both show that Canaanite and Philistine populations remained in portions of the allotted land. Dan later established a northern center at Laish/Tel Dan. Levi received cities among the tribes rather than one contiguous territorial inheritance, while Joseph’s inheritance was represented through Ephraim and Manasseh; Manasseh itself had land on both sides of the Jordan.

Key places

Anchors for reading Joshua and Judges.

Shiloh

The tabernacle and central sanctuary were established here, and the remaining western allotments were distributed from this setting.

Joshua 18:1–10; 19:51

Shechem

A central hill-country site tied to covenant renewal and later designated as a city of refuge.

Joshua 20:7; 24:1–28

Hebron

Major Judahite hill-country city associated with Caleb and later with David’s first capital.

Joshua 14:13–15; 20:7

Jericho

The first major fortified city encountered after Israel crossed the Jordan into Canaan.

Joshua 5–6

Jerusalem

A border-area city in the Judah/Benjamin descriptions that remained contested during the settlement period.

Joshua 15:8,63; 18:28; Judges 1:21

Dan / Laish

The tribe of Dan struggled in its original western allotment and later seized Laish in the far north, renaming it Dan.

Joshua 19:40–48; Judges 18

Reference approach

Scripture first, geography cross-checked.

Primary boundary texts are Numbers 32 and Joshua 13–20. Geographic presentation is cross-checked with Bible Mapper’s “Tribal Allotments of the Promised Land” and its discussion of land still unconquered by the end of Saul’s reign. Key site coordinates are cross-checked with OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding, including Shiloh at Khirbet Seilun, Shechem at Tell Balatah, Hebron at Tel Rumeida, Jerusalem, Beersheba at Tel Beer Sheva, and Dan at Tel Dan.
