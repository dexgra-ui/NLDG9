The World of the Bible

Orient the whole biblical story geographically. This map spans Egypt and the Levant, Mesopotamia and Persia, Asia Minor and Greece, and finally Rome. It is designed as a reference frame for the rest of the NLDG atlas rather than as a snapshot of one political moment.

Accuracy note: This overview intentionally avoids drawing one set of political borders across thousands of years. Egypt, Assyria, Babylonia, Persia, Israel/Judah, Greek kingdoms, and Rome controlled different territory at different times. The shaded areas below are broad geographic orientation regions, not claims about one empire’s exact boundary.
Think in layers of time. The Old Testament story is centered primarily in the Ancient Near East, while Acts and the New Testament letters expand the geographic frame westward through Asia Minor, Macedonia, Greece, and Italy. The same city can also carry different significance in different biblical periods.

The biblical world at a glance

Seven geographic zones that keep appearing in Scripture.

Egypt & the Nile

Egypt shapes the stories of Abraham, Joseph, Moses, the Exodus, the monarchy’s international politics, and Jesus’ infancy.

Genesis 12; 37–50 • Exodus 1–15 • Matthew 2

Canaan / Israel / Judah

The central land of the patriarchs, conquest, judges, monarchy, prophets, and Jesus’ earthly ministry.

Genesis–Kings • Prophets • Gospels

Mesopotamia

The Tigris–Euphrates world includes the settings of Ur, Harran, Assyria, Nineveh, and Babylon.

Genesis 11–12 • 2 Kings 17; 24–25 • Daniel

Persia

The Persian Empire provides the setting for the return from exile and for Esther, Ezra, and Nehemiah.

Ezra • Nehemiah • Esther

Asia Minor

Modern Turkey contains many cities central to Paul’s journeys and the churches addressed in Acts, the Epistles, and Revelation.

Acts 13–20 • Galatians • Ephesians • Revelation 2–3

Macedonia & Greece

Philippi, Thessalonica, Berea, Athens, and Corinth become major settings as the gospel moves west.

Acts 16–20 • Philippians • Thessalonians • Corinthians

Rome & Italy

Rome forms the western endpoint of Acts and the political center behind much of the New Testament’s imperial context.

Acts 27–28 • Romans

Reference approach

A framework for every other NLDG map.

OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding provides mapped identifications for biblical places across the canon, and its current data identifies locations such as Jerusalem, Babylon, Antioch on the Orontes, Ephesus, and Rome with very high confidence. Bible Mapper’s WebViewer likewise treats the “biblical world” as a broad navigable geographic canvas onto which places and biblical references can be layered. NLDG uses that same reference principle here: secure city anchors plus broad geographic orientation, without pretending one boundary map can represent every biblical era.
