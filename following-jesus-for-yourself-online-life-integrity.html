Preteen & Teen Bible Studies · Lesson 7 of 10

Online Life and Integrity.

The internet does not create a separate version of discipleship. Jesus is Lord over what we text, post, scroll, watch, comment, and do when no one else seems to be watching.

📖 Ephesians 4:25–32Philippians 4:81 Corinthians 10:3135–50 minutesAges 11–18

Big Question

Does following Jesus change the way I text, post, scroll, watch, comment, and communicate online?

Big Truth

Jesus is Lord over our online life too.

Integrity means digital choices should reflect truth, love, wisdom, self-control, and respect for other people’s dignity.

Open Your Bible

Bring digital life under biblical wisdom.

  1. Ephesians 4:25–32 — Notice speech, anger, truth, harm, kindness, and forgiveness.
  2. Philippians 4:8 — Ask how this might shape what we repeatedly give our attention to.
  3. 1 Corinthians 10:31 — Notice how ordinary activities can be brought under the desire to honor God.

1

Screens can hide consequences without removing them.

It is easier to say something harsh when you cannot see a face, share a rumor when it feels like “just reposting,” or lose hours when apps are designed to keep attention.

Digital actions still affect real people and real character.

2

Truth matters online.

Before sharing something, ask: Do I know this is true? Am I giving the whole story? Is the source trustworthy? Am I spreading something private that is not mine to share?

Being first is not more important than being truthful.

3

Attention shapes us.

Philippians 4:8 does not tell us to ignore everything difficult. Scripture itself faces suffering, injustice, sin, and grief. But what we repeatedly dwell on matters.

Algorithms learn what keeps us watching. Following Jesus means learning what our attention is becoming attached to.

4

Integrity includes what you do when no one is watching.

Private messages, hidden accounts, searches, deleted posts, gaming chats, and anonymous comments can feel invisible. They still form character.

The deeper question is not, “Will someone catch me?” but, “Am I becoming the same kind of person in private that I claim to be in public?”

5

Wisdom includes limits.

Technology can be useful, creative, educational, and connecting. It can also become controlling. A boundary might mean charging a phone outside the bedroom, turning off notifications, limiting an app, avoiding certain content, taking a break, or asking for accountability.

Boundaries are tools for using technology without letting it use you.

Think It Through

Just because you can share it does not mean you should.

If a screenshot of a private conversation starts spreading, what should a Christian ask before forwarding it—even if the screenshot is real?

If an account repeatedly leaves you angry, jealous, insecure, or cruel, what might wisdom look like?

Talk About It

Questions for discussion.

  1. Which parts of Ephesians 4 seem especially relevant online?
  2. Why do people sometimes act differently behind a screen?
  3. Why does reposting a rumor still matter morally?
  4. How can algorithms influence habits and emotions?
  5. What is the difference between privacy and secrecy?
  6. What kinds of digital boundaries can be healthy?
  7. How should Christians respond after a public online mistake?

Do not reveal passwords, browsing history, private messages, explicit content, or account details in a group. You may answer generally or pass.

Preteen Track · roughly 11–14

Use the THINK check.

  • T — Is it true?
  • H — Is it helpful?
  • I — Is it appropriate to share?
  • N — Is it necessary?
  • K — Is it kind?

The questions slow us down before we act impulsively.

Teen Deeper Dive · roughly 14–18

What is a platform training you to become?

Online platforms often reward outrage, certainty, comparison, performance, and constant attention. Which habits increase wisdom, patience, and compassion? Which make you more reactive, envious, distracted, or cruel?

Christian witness online includes fact-checking, humility, integrity, and respectful disagreement—not just posting Bible verses.

Try It This Week

Do a seven-day digital attention audit.

At the end of each day, note what took most of your screen time, what left you grateful or informed, what left you angry, empty, jealous, tempted, or distracted, and one adjustment that might help.

Do not use the audit to shame yourself. Use it to make wiser choices.

Memory Verse

1 Corinthians 10:31

Learn the verse from the Bible translation your family, church, or class uses.

Prayer

Bring your digital life under Christ.

Jesus, You are Lord of my online life too. Help me tell the truth, protect other people’s dignity, control my anger, and refuse gossip and cruelty. Give me wisdom about what I watch and what I allow to shape my attention. Help me use technology as a tool rather than letting it control me. Make my private choices and public witness consistent. Amen.

Leader / Parent Note

Teach digital discipleship without fearmongering.

Purpose: Technology is a tool; the moral questions involve how it is designed, used, and allowed to shape attention and relationships.

Protect privacy

Do not ask students to reveal browsing history, private messages, passwords, explicit content, or personal account details in a group. Encourage private conversation with a trusted adult when support is needed.

Safeguarding

If a student reports cyberbullying, sexual exploitation, threats, coercion, image-based abuse, stalking, or contact from an unsafe adult, take it seriously and follow safeguarding procedures and applicable reporting requirements. Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep.

Avoid simplistic technology rules

Help students develop discernment, limits, truthfulness, and accountability rather than treating every platform or device as equally harmful.

This study does not collect account names, passwords, messages, images, browsing history, names, ages, schools, locations, or contact details.
