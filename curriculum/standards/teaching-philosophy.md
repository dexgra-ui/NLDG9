# Teaching Philosophy

## We seek transformation, not information alone

Biblical knowledge matters, but the purpose of teaching is faithful obedience and Christlike formation. Lessons should move participants from understanding to response.

## We teach Christ, not merely topics

Every subject should be connected to the person, character, teaching, mission, death, resurrection, and lordship of Jesus Christ.

## Scripture is our authority

History, scholarship, experience, and cultural analysis can clarify a lesson, but Scripture remains the final authority for faith and practice.

## We welcome honest questions

Faith is not threatened by sincere questions. Leaders should create space for doubt, curiosity, disagreement, and careful study without allowing discussion to become unkind or detached from Scripture.

## We pursue wisdom rather than fear

Modern issues should be approached with truth, courage, humility, discernment, and hope. Lessons must avoid panic, sensationalism, and partisan manipulation.

## We form disciples who make disciples

Every lesson should help participants grow personally and prepare them to encourage, mentor, teach, or serve someone else.

## We teach with humility

Leaders are learners first. It is appropriate to say, “I do not know. Let us study that together.”

## We measure success by obedience

A successful lesson is not one that merely impresses people or completes every section. It is one that helps people follow Jesus more faithfully.

## The Three Guiding Questions

Every lesson must clearly answer:

1. What does God say?
2. What does this mean?
3. How do we faithfully live this today?
