Accuracy First

A family tree is only as good as its labels.

Some biblical names clearly identify individual people. Others function as ancestor names, nations, clans, titles, or leadership names. Some relationships are explicit; a few are probable from immediate context; manuscript traditions sometimes preserve variant forms. Repeated names are kept separate when Scripture does not explicitly identify them as the same person.

Scripture-stated

A parent, spouse, child, sibling, concubine, clan, or other connection is recorded as explicit only when the biblical text states it.

Probability stays labeled

When a relationship is strongly suggested but not directly worded, the record says probable instead of pretending the inference is a quotation from Scripture.

Variants and identities stay visible

Textual variants, different genealogical presentations, titles, and repeated-name questions are not “fixed” by choosing one spelling or merging two people without evidence. The uncertainty remains part of the reference.

Roadmap

This becomes the whole-Bible reference one verified section at a time.

Phase 1 · Genesis 1–11

Primeval families, Noah, Table of Nations, Shem to Terah. Entered.

Phase 2 · Genesis 12–50

Patriarchal families, Edom and Seir, Joseph, and the family entering Egypt. Entered.

Phase 3 · Exodus–Deuteronomy

Moses’ generation, Aaronic and Levitical lines, tribal leaders, spies, census families, wilderness figures, and land-allocation leaders. Entered.

Phase 4 · Joshua–Ruth

Conquest-era leaders, named judges and rulers, Samson, the Danite priestly line, and Ruth’s family line into David. Entered.

Phase 5 · 1 Samuel–2 Kings

Next: Samuel and Eli’s families, Saul’s house, David’s complete family, royal dynasties of Judah and Israel, named prophets, officials, military leaders, and every stated family connection through the fall of the kingdoms.

Later phases
