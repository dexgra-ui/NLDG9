# Leader and Writing Standards

## Leader Standards

A leader should be equipped to:

- Pray and study before teaching.
- State the lesson’s big idea clearly.
- Explain the passage in context.
- Distinguish Scripture from opinion or dramatization.
- Ask questions before supplying answers.
- Include quieter participants without pressuring them.
- Redirect unproductive debate with grace.
- Admit when further study is needed.
- Recognize situations requiring safeguarding or qualified support.
- End with hope, application, and prayer.

## Leading Like Jesus

- Shepherd people rather than manage a meeting.
- Use authority as stewardship, not ownership.
- Listen before assuming.
- Correct without humiliating.
- Protect the dignity and safety of participants.
- Measure success by growth and obedience, not by finishing every page.

## Writing Standards

- Write in clear, natural language.
- Prefer direct sentences and concrete examples.
- Explain theological terms instead of displaying expertise.
- Avoid filler, repetition, clichés, partisan slogans, and fear-based language.
- Do not present speculation as fact.
- Attribute quotations and outside research accurately.
- Use consistent Scripture references and translation labels.
- Make applications specific enough to practice.
- Preserve a tone of truth, grace, dignity, and hope.

## The Chosen and Other Creative Media

When a study uses a dramatization:

- State what comes directly from Scripture.
- Identify invented dialogue, backstory, timeline compression, or composite scenes.
- Explain how the creative choice may help viewers imagine the setting.
- Remind participants that Scripture remains the authority.

## Accessibility

- Use descriptive headings.
- Avoid relying on color alone.
- Provide meaningful link text and image descriptions.
- Keep printable versions readable in black and white.
- Design questions and activities for varied reading and experience levels.
