Preteen & Teen Bible Studies · Lesson 10 of 10

Purpose, Gifts, and Living Faith Out Loud.

God’s purpose is bigger than discovering one perfect job or dramatic calling. You can belong to Christ, serve faithfully, grow your gifts, and take the next right step even while your future is still unfolding.

📖 Romans 12:1–81 Peter 4:8–11Matthew 5:13–16Micah 6:840–55 minutes

Big Question

How do I know what God wants me to do with my life?

Big Truth

God’s purpose is bigger than finding one perfect future job.

Every follower of Jesus is called to love God, become more like Christ, serve others, use gifts faithfully, and live with justice, mercy, humility, and courage wherever God places them.

Open Your Bible

Start with your whole life, not one future role.

  1. Romans 12:1–8 — Notice that offering your whole life to God comes before discussion of individual gifts.
  2. 1 Peter 4:8–11 — Look for the connection between gifts and serving people.
  3. Matthew 5:13–16 — Notice what Jesus says about visible faith and good works.
  4. Micah 6:8 — Pay attention to this simple, deep summary of faithful living.

1

Purpose begins with belonging to God.

Young people can feel pressure to discover a perfect school, career, talent, ministry, relationship, or achievement. Romans 12 begins deeper: offer yourself to God, let Him transform your thinking, and learn to serve faithfully with what He has given you.

Your purpose is not fragile enough to disappear because one plan changes.

2

Gifts are for service, not status.

Romans 12 and 1 Peter 4 move the question from “Which gift makes me special?” to “How can what God has given me strengthen other people?”

Some lead publicly. Others organize, listen, teach, create, give, encourage, repair, welcome, protect, build, or quietly notice needs. Faithfulness matters more than visibility.

3

You do not need to know your whole future to obey today.

You may not know your career ten years from now. You can still practice honesty today. You can love your neighbor, work diligently, pray, learn, and serve now.

Waiting for a dramatic calling can become an excuse to ignore ordinary obedience.

4

Living faith out loud does not mean performing religion.

Jesus calls His followers light in the world, and He also warns against religious acts done merely to be seen.

Christian witness is not constant self-promotion. It is character, compassion, courage, truthfulness, and good works that point beyond the self toward God. Sometimes witness includes speaking about Jesus; sometimes it looks like honest work, service, defending someone being mistreated, or admitting a mistake.

5

Purpose includes justice, mercy, and humility.

Micah 6:8 keeps “calling” from becoming only a conversation about personal dreams. God cares about how we treat people.

Ambition is not automatically wrong, but ambition must come under Christ. Ask not only, “How successful can I become?” but, “Who might be helped by what I am becoming?”

6

Your gifts can change and grow.

You may discover abilities over time. Some gifts strengthen through practice, and different seasons call for different kinds of service.

Do not compare your beginning to someone else’s mature skill. Growth requires patience, feedback, work, and humility.

Think It Through

Purpose is not the same as visibility.

If someone believes their only meaningful purpose would be becoming famous, leading a huge ministry, or having a highly respected career, what might Romans 12 and 1 Peter 4 say?

If someone says, “I am not naturally talented, so God probably cannot use me,” what is missing from that conclusion?

Talk About It

Questions for discussion.

  1. Why does Romans 12 begin with offering our whole lives to God?
  2. What is the difference between a gift and a status symbol?
  3. Why is ordinary faithfulness important when the future is uncertain?
  4. How can Christians live their faith visibly without performing for attention?
  5. What does Micah 6:8 add to our understanding of purpose?
  6. Why can comparison hide opportunities to serve?
  7. What kind of character matters regardless of career or calling?

You do not have to reveal private future plans, family pressures, finances, health information, or personal fears to participate.

Preteen Track · roughly 11–14

I ENJOY · I AM LEARNING · PEOPLE NEED

Make three lists: things you enjoy, skills you are developing, and ways people around you could be helped. Look for one small place where the lists overlap.

That might mean helping a younger student, creating something encouraging, assisting at church, cleaning without being asked, listening to a lonely person, or practicing a skill so you can use it well later.

Teen Deeper Dive · roughly 14–18

Calling · career · gifts · vocation.

A career is paid work. A gift is an ability or Spirit-enabled capacity used in service. Calling includes the broad Christian call to belong to Christ and live faithfully, along with particular responsibilities God may give in different seasons.

What happens when someone treats one career choice as if a wrong decision will permanently destroy God’s purpose? How can ambition be redirected toward excellent work, service, stewardship, and love of neighbor?

Try It This Week

Use something you already have to serve.

Choose one small act of service using time, attention, strength, creativity, knowledge, encouragement, money, organization, technology, or practical skill. Do it without posting about it unless sharing is genuinely helpful.

Then ask what you learned, what felt natural, what required growth, and who could give honest feedback about your gifts.

Memory Verse

1 Peter 4:10

Learn the verse from the Bible translation your family, church, or class uses.

Prayer

Take the next faithful step.

Jesus, my life belongs to You. Help me stop measuring purpose by fame, attention, or comparison. Show me how to use what You have given me to serve others faithfully. Grow my gifts, correct my motives, and give me courage to work hard without making achievement my identity. Teach me to do justice, love mercy, walk humbly, and let my life point toward You. Amen.

Leader / Parent Note

End the series with a broad biblical vision of calling.

Purpose: Do not pressure students to identify one dramatic life plan.

Use humility about the future

Avoid telling a young person that you know God has called them to a specific career, ministry role, marriage, mission field, or outcome. Adults can recognize gifts and opportunities without claiming certainty about God’s private plan for someone else.

Do not rank careers spiritually

Faithful work in ordinary fields is not less spiritual than visible ministry work.

Honor real limitations and differences

Be sensitive to disability, chronic illness, financial limits, family responsibilities, or fewer opportunities. Purpose should never be framed as though only highly visible, physically demanding, academically elite, or financially successful paths matter.

Respect Christian differences

Where churches differ about particular spiritual gifts, acknowledge the differences fairly and keep the emphasis on serving others faithfully under Scripture. If a safety concern surfaces in discussion, follow safeguarding procedures and applicable reporting requirements. Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep.

This study does not ask young people to submit future plans, financial information, health details, names, ages, schools, locations, photos, or contact information.
