God Gave Me Gifts to Help Others.

Different gifts are not trophies. God gives people different abilities and opportunities so we can serve, grow together, and bring Him glory.

📖 1 Corinthians 12:4–7, 12–27Romans 12:4–81 Peter 4:10Mark 10:4520–30 minutesAges 7–11

Big Question

Why has God given people different gifts and abilities?

Big Truth

God gives different gifts, abilities, opportunities, and ways to serve.

No one person has everything, and no gift makes someone more valuable than another person. God calls us to use what He has given us to serve others, build up the church, and bring Him glory.

Open Your Bible

Read and notice.

  1. 1 Corinthians 12:4–7 and 12–27 — Notice the picture Paul uses to describe believers working together.
  2. 1 Peter 4:10 — Look for what we are told to do with the gifts we receive.
  3. Mark 10:45 — Notice how Jesus connects greatness with serving.

1

Different does not mean less important.

Paul compares the church to a body. A body has eyes, ears, hands, feet, and many other parts. They are different, but they belong together.

Imagine if an eye said, “Hands are useless because they cannot see.” That would be silly. The hand was not designed to do the eye’s job.

People are like that too. One child may explain ideas clearly. Another notices when someone is lonely. Another is creative, organized, encouraging, thoughtful, patient, or comfortable leading.

Different gifts can work together.

2

Gifts are not trophies.

It is easy to turn abilities into a contest: who sings best, runs fastest, gets the highest grade, gets the solo, has the most followers, or is chosen first?

Competition can sometimes be fun and healthy, but our abilities are not proof that we are better people.

Remember:

A gift is something to steward, not a reason to look down on others.

3

Some gifts are easy to notice. Others are quiet.

Public gifts often receive attention. A person speaking on stage may be noticed more quickly than the person who prepared the room, welcomed a nervous child, prayed faithfully, cleaned afterward, or checked on someone who was absent.

God sees service that other people miss. You do not need to become famous for your life to matter.

4

Gifts grow with practice and character.

Sometimes children think they must discover one perfect “special gift” immediately. You do not.

You are still growing. You can try different things, receive feedback, practice, learn, and notice where God gives you opportunities to help.

Character matters too. Talent without humility can hurt people. Leadership without listening can become controlling. Knowledge without love can become prideful.

We want our abilities and our character to grow together.

5

Jesus used His greatness to serve.

Mark 10 says Jesus came not to be served but to serve. That turns the world’s idea of greatness upside down.

If you are a strong reader, you can help someone practice. If you understand a game, you can explain it patiently. If you are good at organizing, you can help a group prepare. If you notice others’ feelings, you can encourage someone.

Gifts become beautiful when they are used in love.

6

Comparison can hide gratitude.

When we stare at someone else’s ability, we may miss what God is growing in us.

You can celebrate another person without deciding their success means your failure. Try saying, “God gave them something good, and I can be thankful for that. God is also teaching me how to serve.”

Let’s Practice

Notice, practice, serve.

N

Notice — What kinds of tasks or needs do you often notice? What do trusted adults say you do well?

P

Practice — Choose one ability to develop with humility. Gifts grow through learning and practice.

S

Serve — Ask, “How can this help someone besides me?”

You do not need an online quiz to discover how God may use you. Real life, Scripture, prayer, community, and service can teach you a lot over time.

Talk About It

Questions for a child and trusted adult.

  1. Why does Paul compare the church to a body?
  2. What happens when one person thinks their gift is more important than everyone else’s?
  3. What are some quiet ways people serve that may not receive attention?
  4. Why should character grow along with ability?
  5. How did Jesus show greatness through service?
  6. How can we celebrate another person’s gifts without comparing ourselves?
  7. Name one ability or interest you could use to help someone.

Try It This Week

Do one hidden service project.

Choose something helpful that does not need applause.

  • straighten a shared space,
  • encourage someone who is discouraged,
  • help prepare for a family or church activity,
  • practice a skill so you can use it well,
  • help a younger child with patience,
  • pray for someone without announcing it.

Afterward, thank God for the opportunity instead of looking for praise.

Memory Verse

1 Peter 4:10

Learn the verse from the Bible translation your family, church, or class uses. Notice that gifts are connected with serving others and being faithful stewards.

Prayer

Ask God for humility and faithfulness.

God, thank You for making people different and giving us ways to serve. Help me not compare myself or act as if my abilities make me better than someone else. Show me what I can learn, practice, and use for good. Give me humility when people notice me and faithfulness when nobody notices. Help me serve like Jesus. Amen.

Parent / Teacher Note

Teach gifts as stewardship, not fixed identity.

Main objective: Help children see gifts through the biblical lenses of stewardship, service, interdependence, humility, and growth rather than celebrity or fixed labels.

Avoid assigning children permanent identities such as “the leader,” “the smart one,” “the shy one,” or “the helper.” Children develop at different rates, and abilities can change. Encourage exploration without forcing performance.

Do not tie spiritual worth to public ministry visibility. Quiet service, practical help, generosity, prayer, listening, creativity, administration, learning, and many other contributions can bless the body of Christ.

Be cautious with spiritual-gift tests for young children. They may be conversation tools, but they should not become rigid labels or replace Scripture, observation, community, and maturity over time.

If service activities involve children, follow appropriate supervision and safeguarding practices. Children should not be placed into adult caregiving or counseling roles.

This study does not request children to submit talent profiles or personal information online.

Suggested closing question: How could one thing you enjoy become a way to serve someone else?
